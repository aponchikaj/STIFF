## LinkedIn

**Who it's for**: B2B, founders, consultants, recruiters, anyone selling to people with job titles. Highest organic reach of any major platform relative to follower count, and the only one where a 500-follower account can regularly hit 50k+ impressions.

### Post types, ranked by what they earn

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Text post with line breaks | Opinions, stories, lessons | The workhorse. One sentence per line or 1–2 line paragraphs; whitespace is a dwell-time tool |
| Document carousel (PDF) | Frameworks, step-by-steps, teardowns | Highest dwell time of any format — every swipe is a signal. 8–12 slides, one idea per slide, big type (30pt+) |
| Text + single image | Story with proof (screenshot, chart, photo) | Faces outperform graphics; native screenshots outperform designed assets |
| Poll | Cheap engagement, audience research | Reach well but attract low-intent engagement; ≤ 1 per 2 weeks |
| Native video | Talking-head lessons | Under 90 seconds, captions mandatory (~80% watch muted). Vertical preferred |

### Format mechanics

- **Length**: 150–300 words for text posts. The "...see more" fold hits after ~2–3 lines (roughly 200 characters) — the text above the fold is your hook and its only job is earning the click, which is itself a ranking signal.
- **Structure that works**: Hook (1–2 lines) → story or argument in short lines → concrete takeaway → question or soft CTA. Skimmable in 10 seconds, rewarding in 60.
- **Hashtags**: Largely decorative now. 0–3 max, at the end. They do not drive meaningful reach.
- **Links**: External links suppress reach an estimated 30–50%. Put the link in the first comment and say so ("Link in comments"), or skip it entirely.
- **Tagging**: Tag people only when they'll plausibly engage. Tagged users who ignore the post are a mild negative signal.

### Algorithm signals

1. **Golden window**: first 60–90 minutes. The post goes to a slice of your network; comments (especially 5+ words) and dwell time decide whether it fans out to 2nd/3rd-degree feeds.
2. **Dwell time** is a first-class signal — long posts and carousels that hold attention beat short posts that get skimmed, even at equal like counts.
3. **Comments > reactions ≫ likes.** A post with 30 substantive comments outperforms one with 300 likes. Ask one specific, answerable question — not "Thoughts?"
4. **Creator replies count.** Replying to every comment in the first hour roughly doubles the comment signal.
5. **Consistency compounds**: the algorithm favors accounts posting 3–5x/week. More than 1 post/day splits your own reach — your posts compete with each other.

### Cadence and timing

- 3–5 posts/week. Tue–Thu strongest; weekends are quiet but low-competition.
- Best windows: 7:30–9:00am and 12:00–1:00pm in your audience's timezone (people check before work and at lunch).
- 15 minutes of commenting on others' posts before and after publishing warms distribution and puts your name in feeds for free.

### What kills reach

External links in the body, engagement pods (detected and penalized), reposting others' content without commentary, more than one post per day, editing a post in the first hour (resets distribution in some cases — write it right the first time).

## X (Twitter)

**Who it's for**: Tech, startups, finance, media, developer tools, anything with a discourse. Fastest feedback loop of any platform — you learn what resonates in hours, which makes it the best idea-testing ground for the repurposing pipeline (test as tweets, expand winners elsewhere).

### Post types, ranked

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single tweet (one sharp idea) | Opinions, one-liners, stats | 70–120 characters gets the most reposts — short enough to read in one glance, quotable |
| Thread | Full arguments, playbooks, stories | 5–12 tweets. Tweet 1 is a pure hook and must stand alone; each tweet must also stand alone (any of them can be someone's entry point) |
| Quote-tweet with a take | Riding a live conversation | Adds your POV to existing distribution; faster follower growth than cold tweeting for small accounts |
| Reply to large accounts | Borrowed reach | A sharp reply on a big account's fresh tweet can out-impress your own timeline 10x. 10–20 quality replies/day is the fastest 0→1k strategy |
| Image/screenshot tweet | Proof, charts, memes | Screenshots of text ("image essays") dodge the length limit and perform well |

### Format mechanics

- **Thread structure**: Tweet 1 = hook + promise ("7 things I learned taking X from 0 to $1M. No. 4 cost me a year:"). Middle tweets = one idea each, front-loaded. Final tweet = summary + CTA (follow, or link — links belong in the last tweet or a reply, never tweet 1).
- **Line breaks** inside tweets increase readability and dwell. One idea per line.
- **Hashtags**: Don't. On X they read as spam and add nothing.
- **Media**: Native images/video outperform link cards. Never post a bare external link with no framing — lowest-performing tweet type that exists.

### Algorithm signals

1. **First 30 minutes decide everything.** Early velocity (engagements per impression) determines whether the tweet leaves your follower graph and enters "For You."
2. **Replies are the heaviest engagement signal**, then reposts, then likes. Tweets that start arguments or invite completion ("What am I missing?") travel furthest.
3. **Profile clicks and follows from a tweet** are strong signals — and the actual point. A tweet with modest likes but many profile visits did its job.
4. **External links are down-weighted.** Verified/paying accounts get a distribution boost in replies and For You.
5. **Negative signals**: "Show fewer," mutes, and blocks quietly cap accounts. Rage-bait works once, then buries you.

### Cadence and timing

- 2–5 originals/day plus 10–20 replies. Volume matters more here than anywhere except TikTok; most tweets die, and that's the model — you're buying lottery tickets with good odds.
- Weekday mornings (8–10am ET) and evenings (7–9pm ET) are the high-traffic windows for US-centric audiences.
- Re-post winners: rewrite (don't verbatim-repeat) your top tweets every 6–8 weeks.

## Instagram

**Who it's for**: Consumer products, visual categories (food, fitness, design, travel, fashion), personal brands with a lifestyle component. B2B works only when repackaged as visually native education (carousels).

### Post types, ranked

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | Reach and new followers | The only format shown broadly to non-followers. 7–30 seconds; the first 3 seconds decide everything |
| Carousel | Saves, depth, authority | 6–10 slides. Slide 1 = hook in big type; slide 2 must create momentum. Instagram re-serves carousels to the same user starting at slide 2 — a second chance at the hook |
| Stories | Retention and conversion of existing followers | Daily. Polls, questions, and sliders build a "reply habit"; story repliers are your warmest leads |
| Single image | Announcements, aesthetic upkeep | Lowest reach format; use sparingly |
| Lives/broadcast channels | Superfan depth | Optional; only once the audience exists |

### Format mechanics

- **Reels**: Vertical 9:16, shot or captioned natively. Hook in the first 1–3 seconds — visual pattern-interrupt plus an on-screen text promise ("3 mistakes killing your ad spend"). On-screen captions mandatory. Loop-friendly endings (the last frame flows into the first) boost rewatch, which reads as retention.
- **Watermarks**: Reels bearing TikTok watermarks are explicitly down-ranked. Re-export clean or re-shoot — never port the watermarked file.
- **Captions**: First 125 characters show before "more" — treat as a second hook. Longer captions (300–500 chars) add dwell time when the post earns it.
- **Hashtags**: Minor discovery value now; 3–5 relevant tags at most. Keyword-rich captions matter more (Instagram search reads caption text).
- **Links**: None clickable in captions. Link in bio, or DM automation ("comment PLAYBOOK and I'll send it") — comment-to-DM flows convert well and generate comment signals simultaneously.

### Algorithm signals

1. **Watch time and completion** on Reels — a 10-second Reel watched twice beats a 60-second Reel abandoned at 15s.
2. **Saves and shares (sends)** are the strongest feed signals — both proxy "this is genuinely useful." Design carousels to be saved: checklists, frameworks, reference material.
3. **Early engagement window** (~first hour) applies here too; Stories posted alongside a feed post funnel your warmest audience into that window.
4. **Sender-to-DM shares** weigh heavily — content people send to a friend ("this is so us," useful how-tos) travels.
5. **Consistency**: accounts posting Reels 3–5x/week get sustained non-follower distribution; sporadic posting resets momentum.

### Cadence and timing

- 3–5 Reels/week + 1–2 carousels/week + daily Stories (3–7 frames).
- Timing matters less than on LinkedIn/X (Reels distribute over days, not hours), but late morning and 7–9pm local perform reliably.

## TikTok

**Who it's for**: Anyone whose buyer is under ~40, consumer anything, and increasingly B2B founders doing "edutainment." The purest content meritocracy: follower count barely matters because every video is independently tested.

### How distribution actually works

Every upload goes to a test cohort of roughly 200–500 viewers regardless of your follower count. If completion rate, rewatches, shares, and likes clear the bar, it goes to a larger cohort, then larger — in steps. Consequences:

- A 0-follower account can hit 1M views on video one.
- A 500k-follower account gets nothing if the video doesn't hold retention.
- Videos can resurface and explode weeks after posting — never delete a "flop" early.

### Post types, ranked

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Native talking-head | Opinions, lessons, storytimes | Direct-to-camera, jump cuts every 2–4 seconds, captions on |
| Screen-share / tutorial | SaaS, tools, how-tos | "Watch me do X in 30 seconds" massively outperforms polished product demos |
| POV / skit | Relatable pain points | "POV: your CFO sees the AWS bill" — pain-point humor is B2B's cheat code here |
| Reply-to-comment video | Compounding engagement | Turns one video's comments into a content series; strong signal loop |
| Trend remix | Riding sounds/formats | Use the trending sound/format, keep your niche's substance. Move within 3–5 days of a trend emerging |

### Format mechanics

- **Length**: 21–34 seconds is the completion-rate sweet spot. Longer (1–3 min) works only when retention graphs stay flat; watch time now feeds monetization but completion still rules distribution.
- **The first 1–2 seconds** are everything: start mid-action or mid-sentence. Never open with "Hey guys" or a logo — cold-open into the payoff's setup.
- **Retention engineering**: tease the payoff up front ("the third one got me fired"), pattern-interrupt every few seconds (cuts, zooms, captions, b-roll), deliver the payoff at the end, loop the ending if possible.
- **Text and sound**: On-screen captions always; trending sounds (even at low volume under speech) give a discovery edge. TikTok search reads spoken words, captions, and text overlays — say your keywords out loud ("SEO for TikTok" is literal).
- **Links**: Bio link only (and only above 1k followers). CTAs drive comments and follows, not clicks — the conversion path is profile → bio → owned channel.

### Algorithm signals

1. **Completion rate** — the dominant signal. Percentage watched beats every other metric.
2. **Rewatches and loops** — a video that loops seamlessly inflates watch time per viewer.
3. **Shares** — strongest quality signal after completion.
4. **Comments** — including argument-bait; reply-with-video multiplies the loop.
5. **Velocity within each test cohort** decides promotion to the next tier; posting when your audience is awake still matters for the first cohort.

### Cadence and timing

- 1–3 videos/day for growth mode; 4–5/week minimum to stay in rotation. TikTok is the highest-volume platform — batch-shoot 5–10 videos in one session.
- Judge trends weekly, not monthly: a trend older than ~2 weeks is already saturated.

## Cross-Platform Timing Reference

All times in the *audience's* dominant timezone. These are starting points — replace with your own analytics after 4 weeks of data.

| Platform | Best days | Best windows | Early-engagement window to babysit |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Tue–Thu | 7:30–9:00am, 12:00–1:00pm | First 60–90 min |
| X | Mon–Fri | 8–10am, 7–9pm | First 30 min |
| Instagram | Tue–Fri | 11am–1pm, 7–9pm | First 60 min (Reels keep distributing for days) |
| TikTok | Any day | 12–3pm, 7–10pm | First 60 min per test cohort; videos can resurface for weeks |

## Repurposing Cut Sheet

One pillar asset (e.g., a 2,000-word blog post or 30-min podcast) maps to:

| Platform | Cuts | Count |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 1 carousel (the framework) + 2 text posts (the story, the contrarian claim) | 3 |
| X | 1 thread (full argument) + 3–4 single tweets (sharpest standalone lines) | 4–5 |
| Instagram | 1 carousel (steps, save-optimized) + 1–2 Reels (strongest 20-second moments, re-shot vertical) | 2–3 |
| TikTok | 2–3 native talking-head videos (one idea each, re-shot — not clipped) | 2–3 |

That's 11–14 posts from one asset, staggered over 2–3 weeks — each native, none identical.
