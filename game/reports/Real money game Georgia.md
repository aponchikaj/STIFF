# Pay for the dare, never for the danger

STIFF can legally run a Nerve-style task game that pays fixed GEL per approved task in Georgia without a gambling permit, an NBG payment licence or AML obliged-entity status, but only while the design holds three lines: rewards are fixed and earned by effort with no element of chance, entry and watching are free, and the in-app balance is funded only by STIFF, never topped up by users, never transferable between them and only redeemable by withdrawal to the player's own account. Cross any one of those lines (a random reward, a paid entry, a bought coin that converts to cash, a P2P transfer) and the project falls into regimes built for casinos and e-money institutions, with permit fees in the hundreds of thousands to millions of GEL and a 25+ age bar for Georgian citizens. Payouts are a solved problem with no new licence needed: STIFF pays its own debt from its own business account using Bank of Georgia's Bonline bulk-transfer API or TBC's BIS batch API to any Georgian IBAN, with Flitt's `p2pcredit` as a later card-payout upgrade. The real costs sit elsewhere. Every payout to an individual is a 20% personal income tax withholding event with monthly per-person reporting, which turns an advertised 0.50 GEL into either 0.40 GEL in the player's pocket or 0.63 GEL of cost to STIFF, and forces ID verification before the first withdrawal. The legal exposure that matters most is not gambling but harm: STIFF designs, rewards and approves every dare, Georgian consumer law voids waivers for gross-negligence injury, advertising law bans content urging dangerous acts, and no Georgian insurer publicly offers cover for an online dare platform. The safe path is an 18+, Georgia-only, web-first launch with a curated task pool that bans physical risk, weekly IBAN batches above a minimum withdrawal threshold, and a written Georgian legal opinion before the first lari moves. Nothing here is legal advice, and several primary texts could not be read in their current consolidated versions; those gaps are flagged where they matter.

## Nerve's engine is worth copying, its coercion is the part to invert

The 2016 film builds a two-sided live market: watchers pay to watch and author and vote on dares, players accept dares one at a time for escalating cash filmed on their own phones, and the most popular players reach a finale ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_(2016_film))). Reviewers describe watcher subscription fees funding the payouts, though the film never shows a rule text or price ([RogerEbert](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/nerve-2016); [Reelviews](https://www.reelviews.net/reelviews/nerve)). Watcher count is the visible score, and watchers also place side bets on players ([PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/review/whos-watching-the-watchers-in-nerve)). Escalation runs from kissing a stranger to riding a motorcycle blindfolded and crossing between buildings on a ladder, each dare carrying a cash prize ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_(2016_film))).

What makes the fictional game coercive is a short list of deliberate rules. **Every dare must be recorded, a player who bails or fails loses everything earned, and a player who reports the game becomes a "Prisoner"** who can recover losses only by winning ([IMDb plot summary](https://imdb.com/title/tt3531824/plotsummary); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_(2016_film))). The game enforces this by reaching into players' finances: when the protagonist reports it, money is removed from the bank account she shares with her mother, and others who try to alert authorities have their families' jobs, accounts and identities compromised. The finale has watchers vote by majority that one player should shoot another, and the game cannot be shut off because watchers' phones form a distributed server; it ends only when watchers are de-anonymised and told they are accessories to murder ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_(2016_film))). The film puts the blame on the anonymous, insatiable crowd more than on the players ([PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/review/whos-watching-the-watchers-in-nerve)).

The Jeanne Ryan novel (2012) differs in one way that matters to a fashion brand: rewards are hand-picked goods from each player's wishlist, starting with designer shoes, rather than cash, and its finale has hidden "wealthy donors" watching through a one-way mirror ([Wikipedia: novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_(Ryan_novel)); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/questions/929814-is-the-movie-nerve-very-different-than)). A wishlist mechanic, where a player pins STIFF items as a target reward, is the most brand-native idea in either version.

The design rule that falls out of this is simple. Keep the engagement layer (live audience, visible counts, a clock, rankings, a finale, paired dares) and invert every mechanic that holds money, identity or reporting hostage, or that lets the crowd set price and danger. STIFF's existing hearts, task clock and watcher voting already map onto the film's engagement layer.

Real precedents show why the inversions matter. Peer-funded dare apps keep appearing and fading: Pickle (UK, 2015) took a 10% commission from both sides and let users post dares such as head-shaving and streaking, reaching about 90,000 users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickle_(app))); Double Dog (Austin, 2016) let targets turn a dare back on its creator, who paid 3x for backing out, with no stated safety rules or age limits ([Built In Austin](https://www.builtinaustin.com/articles/double-dog-dare-cash)), and its successor says "app store issues" shut it down, an unverified self-report ([Double Dog site](https://doubledogapp.com/dares-for-money/)). The closest precedent to STIFF is not a dare app at all. **TikTok Lite Rewards paid points, exchangeable for Amazon vouchers, for in-app tasks; the European Commission opened Digital Services Act proceedings in April 2024 over its addictive effect and risk to minors, and TikTok withdrew it from the EU permanently in August 2024** ([EU Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/tiktok-commits-permanently-withdraw-tiktok-lite-rewards-programme-eu-comply-digital-services-act); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/05/tiktok-lite-eu-closes-addictive-design-case-after-tiktok-commits-to-not-bring-back-rewards-mechanism)). That case turned on addiction and minors, not gambling. In *Anderson v. TikTok* (3rd Cir., 2024), a platform's own recommendation of the "Blackout Challenge" to a 10-year-old who died was held outside Section 230 protection, letting a negligence claim proceed ([3rd Cir. opinion](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/223061p.pdf)). An operator that selects and pushes tasks owns them. Beast Games contestants filed an unresolved 2024 class action alleging mistreatment, misstated odds and unpaid prizes ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/lawsuit-targets-maker-amazon-prime-game-show-mrbeast-claiming-contesta-rcna171624)), and a French streamer died in August 2025 on a Kick stream after months of filmed abuse, prompting a prosecutor's investigation ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/man-dies-livestream-jean-pormanove-raphael-graven-france)).

App stores add their own rules. Apple bans apps that urge challenges risking physical harm (1.4.5), requires developer-sponsored contests with in-app official rules disclaiming Apple's involvement (5.3.1 to 5.3.2), and bars in-app purchase of credit for real-money gaming (5.3.3) ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google Play bars unlicensed apps where users stake money, or items bought with money, for a prize of real-world value, and bars calls to action for real-money contests ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)). Neither store explicitly addresses fixed cash pay for completed tasks outside a contest, so that reading is untested. Running the GEL side as a web app or PWA, which STIFF already operates, avoids store review for the riskiest part.

## Two working payout rails exist today, and neither needs a licence

The simplest compliant route is **bank-to-IBAN transfers from STIFF's own business account via the bank's corporate API**. Bank of Georgia's Bonline API accepts a bulk domestic "package" (`POST api/documents/bulk/domestic`) of IBAN payments, each with beneficiary name, personal ID, amount, purpose and a GUID; payments to other banks are allowed by passing the RTGS code, BULK dispatch is capped at **10,000 GEL per transaction**, and a duplicate `UniqueId` within 7 days is blocked, which gives idempotency for free ([BoG bulk-domestic](https://api.bog.ge/docs/en/bonline/documents/bulk-domestic)). TBC's BIS Transfers API offers single and batch transfer endpoints under OAuth 2.0, with "final execution" subject to signing rules in Business Internet Banking ([TBC BIS](https://developers.tbcbank.ge/docs/bis-transfers)); its older SOAP service imports 1 to 1,000 orders per batch ([TBC batch import](https://developers.tbcbank.ge/docs/import-batch-payments)). Both banks therefore give STIFF a "maker in code, checker in the bank UI" control: the backend builds the batch, and the finance owner signs it.

Card payouts are the second rail. **Flitt (TBC group, formerly Fondy Georgia) markets "instant withdrawals to users" and "online balance withdrawals" to a card or account** ([Flitt Payouts](https://flitt.com/payouts/)), via `POST /api/p2pcredit`, which requires a card token from an earlier purchase made with `required_rectoken=Y`, not a raw card number, and is signed with a separate payout key ([Flitt docs](https://docs.flitt.com/api/create-order-p2pcredit/)). Onboarding requires a Georgian-registered business and an active TBC account ([Flitt Payouts](https://flitt.com/payouts/)). The token requirement means a player must first make a small card payment, such as a shop purchase or a verification charge, before withdrawing to that card. Whether Flitt pushes to other banks' cards is undocumented.

For paying from its own account, STIFF gains nothing from it.

Pricing is the weak point of the evidence. Almost no payout fee is public. The only hard numbers are the BoG 10,000 GEL BULK cap, an NBG interbank bulk clearing fee of 0.2 GEL in the 09:00 to 17:45 window, which is the central bank's charge and not a retail tariff ([NBG fees](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/working-hours-and-service-fees)), and Keepz's 0.50 GEL. Against a 0.50 GEL easy-task reward, any per-transfer fee in that range makes small withdrawals uneconomic, so a **minimum withdrawal threshold (10 to 20 GEL) and weekly batching** are necessary, not optional. Interbank bulk transfers settle in NBG clearing windows, so promise same or next business day, not instant. Banks KYC STIFF, not its payees; the bank checks only IBAN format, with BoG's `CheckInn` matching personal ID to account only for BoG recipients ([BoG bulk-domestic](https://api.bog.ge/docs/en/bonline/documents/bulk-domestic)). STIFF must verify the holder itself. The NBG is actively fining weak AML in this sector, 14,000 GEL against CPay and 39,000 GEL against PayUnicard in 2025 ([Quickpay.ge](https://quickpay.ge/en/blog/georgian-payment-gateways-comparison)), so partners will scrutinise STIFF's controls.

## Three regimes stay closed only while three design lines hold

### Gambling and prize games: fixed rewards for effort fall outside the law

The governing statute is the Law on Organising Lotteries, Games of Chance and Other Prize Games (No. 1180, 2005), consolidated to 28 October 2026 ([Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)). Its categories all turn on chance: a gambling game's outcome depends wholly or partly on chance with possible cash winnings (Art. 3(a)); a lottery draws a prize fund publicly by chance; prize games are lotto, bingo and totalizator, the last being bets on an event's outcome; and a **promotional draw** is a free chance-based draw by a product's producer, where **charging any participation fee is not permitted**. **Article 4 excludes games decided by skill, knowledge or dexterity with no element of chance.** Every gambling or prize game, including online, needs a permit (Art. 5), and running an unauthorised lottery is fined GEL 50,000, then GEL 150,000 per repeat (Art. 37²) ([Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)).

A fixed GEL amount for an approved task involves no draw, no prize fund and no stake, so it fits the Art. 4 exclusion well. Review of hand-ins is a judgment of performance, not chance, but only if the approval criteria are published and objective. The tripwires are specific. A season or sponsor prize allocated by draw is a promotional draw needing a Revenue Service permit, reported at **GEL 15,000 for one year**, with approved rules, a draw committee and results reported within two days ([GC Authority](https://gcauthority.com/markets/georgia/); [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)); the fee figure is secondary and unverified against the fee law. A tie-break by lot reintroduces chance. Paying voters only when they match the majority starts to resemble a totalizator, so pay a fixed amount per vote or none. And **paid coins that buy a chance at cash or prizes create stake plus chance plus prize**, which a promotional-draw permit cannot cover because paid entry is barred; that lands STIFF in the full gambling regime, where Andersen reports online casino permits at GEL 5,000,000 a year, says unapproved "skill-chance hybrid products" are prohibited ([Legal 500 / Andersen](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/georgia-gambling-law/)), Georgian citizens under 25 cannot play, and gambling advertising has been banned since March 2022 ([Justice League](https://www.justiceleague.ge/blog/gambling-industry); [identomat](https://www.identomat.com/compliance-guide/guide/igaming-gambling-compliance-in-georgia)). No Georgian regulator or court has classified a dare app as gambling; the risk analysis rests on the statute's definitions.

### Payment services and e-money: the balance is STIFF's debt, not users' money

Under the Law on Payment Systems and Payment Services, only an NBG-registered PSP, a bank, a microfinance organisation or an exempt person may provide payment services, and issuing e-money is one (Arts. 13, 15) ([Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)). The definition of e-money has two limbs: value **received from users**, and **accepted as a means of payment by persons other than the issuer** ([Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)). A balance that STIFF alone funds and alone redeems fails both, so it is better characterised as accrued remuneration STIFF owes under the task contract. The law is current to an October 2026 amendment that rewrote Art. 18 safeguarding and appeals but did not touch e-money or the exclusions ([Matsne, Law 1890](https://matsne.gov.ge/document/view/7023423)). There is **no express loyalty or limited-network exclusion**; the nearest carve-out covers instruments usable only on the issuer's premises, and Art. 13(3) lets the NBG add more.

The position breaks the moment users can top up the balance, send it to each other, or spend it with third parties. Bought coins that can be cashed out become user-funded stored value. Becoming a PSP would mean NBG registration, segregated nominal accounts, 15-year records and capital that sources cannot agree on: GEL 50,000 to 350,000 by scope ([BGI Legal](https://bgi.ge/files/BGI%20Law%20Brief%20_%20May%202023.pdf)), a flat GEL 250,000 ([licentium](https://www.licentium.io/hub/georgia)), or around GEL 1,000,000 for e-money ([legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/banking-and-finance-law-en/e-money-regulation-en)). That is disproportionate for a fashion brand. If balances grow large or paid features arrive, the right move is a licensed bank or PSP partner holding balances in a safeguarded account, though no Georgian provider was found offering a white-label wallet for this.

### AML: probably outside the list, inside the bank's expectations

The 2019 AML Law, consolidated to April 2026, lists organisers of lotteries, gambling and other prize games, and PSPs, as obliged entities ([Matsne 4690334](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4690334); [MONEYVAL](https://rm.coe.int/moneyval-2024-22-ge-5thround-3rdenhfur/1680b2c262)). A skill-based reward game with no permit and no PSP status is probably not on that list. Obtaining a promotional-draw permit could make STIFF an "organiser of other prize games" and bring obligations such as CDD above GEL 5,000 per transaction, suspicious-transaction reporting, an AML officer and sanctions screening ([identomat](https://www.identomat.com/compliance-guide/guide/igaming-gambling-compliance-in-georgia); [Legal 500 / Andersen](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/georgia-gambling-law/)). Even outside the list, STIFF should run voluntary KYC, name matching between player and account holder, velocity limits and multi-account detection, because tax reporting requires the identity anyway and multi-accounting is the realistic fraud.

## Every payout is a 20% tax event and a personal-data file

### Tax: classify as payment for services, and decide who bears the 20%

Georgia has no prize exemption that fits a company's task reward. A legal person paying an individual who is not a registered entrepreneur for services withholds **20% PIT** at source (Arts. 81(1), 154(1)(d)), the same rate that applies to promotional-game winnings (Art. 154(1)(e)). The tax is remitted on payment and reported by the 15th of the following month with each recipient's **personal registration number, name, address, income and tax withheld** (Art. 154(3)) ([Tax Code, 2023 English consolidation](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)). A payer that fails to withhold owes the tax itself with penalties. The lottery exemption up to GEL 1,000 (Art. 82(1)(w)) does not reach task rewards, and the GEL 1,000 gift exemption applies only to gifts from natural persons. Framing rewards as gifts is the worst option: it lifts STIFF's withholding below GEL 1,000 per person but leaves the player liable and makes the transfer a profit-tax object at an effective **17.65%** (15% on the payout divided by 0.85, Arts. 97(1)(c), 97(10)). Framing them as payment for content STIFF licenses for marketing makes them an ordinary business expense with no profit tax. Cash payouts carry no VAT, but merchandise rewards can be a deemed VAT supply, and the GEL 50 "gift of little value" carve-out excludes advertising expenses. The text read dates from 2023; the October 2026 consolidation was not checked.

| Basis | Advertised | Tax withheld | Player receives | STIFF cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross (player bears tax) | 0.50 | 0.10 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
| Net (STIFF grosses up, net / 0.8) | 0.50 | about 0.13 | 0.50 | about 0.63 |
| Gross | 100.00 | 20.00 | 80.00 | 100.00 |
| Net | 100.00 | 25.00 | 100.00 | 125.00 |

The gross-up must divide by 0.8; adding 20% on top under-withholds. Rounding a 0.625 GEL gross per task drifts, so compute tax on monthly per-person totals, which is the reporting unit anyway. The UI must say which basis applies ("0.50 GEL, tax paid by STIFF" or "0.50 GEL before 20% tax"), since the Advertising Law bans unreliable claims about price and terms. Non-residents performing tasks abroad likely have no Georgian-source income (Art. 104), while those performing in Georgia may face 10% under Art. 134(1)(e) ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/georgia/corporate/withholding-taxes)); that split is unsettled, which is another reason to geo-restrict paid tasks to Georgia. Virtual Zone and International Company status are built for IT exporters billing foreign clients and do not help here ([IBCCS](https://ibccs.tax/blog/virtual-zone-vs-international-company-status-georgia/)). A separate game LLC is a legitimate liability ring-fence but duplicates withholding filings and makes intra-group funding a taxable service.

### Personal data: a GDPR-like law with modest fines but real duties

The Personal Data Protection Law No. 3144 (2023, main provisions in force 1 March 2024) tracks the GDPR: ten lawful bases, own consent from age 16 with parental consent below, biometric data as a special category, a **72-hour breach notice**, DPIAs for high-risk processing and a DPO for controllers that systematically monitor behaviour, notified within 10 working days ([PDP Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)). STIFF's lawful bases are contract for accounts, submissions and payouts, legal obligation for the tax identity data, and consent for marketing reuse of player videos. **A selfie or face-match liveness check is biometric**, triggering written purpose and retention rules and a DPIA, so bank-name matching or manual ID review is the lighter choice. A game that scores players, runs anti-cheat and queues reviews arguably meets the "systematic monitoring" DPO trigger; appointing an outsourced DPO is cheap insurance. Hosting on Supabase, Fly.io and Cloudflare is a cross-border transfer, lawful to adequate jurisdictions or on listed grounds, with **contract-based transfers needing a Service permit** and a processor agreement required regardless. Fines are low, GEL 1,000 to 10,000 per violation and capped at GEL 20,000 per proceeding for larger entities, but aggravated for minors' data ([PDP Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)). Targeting EU residents would bring the GDPR itself into play ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). Versions of the law after 2023 were not read.

## STIFF owns every dare it publishes, and no ToS clause transfers that

### Minors: run the money version 18+

Majority in Georgia is 18, and 7 to 18 year olds have limited capacity; a minor's transaction needs a legal representative's consent unless it only brings a benefit ([Civil Code Arts. 12, 14, 15](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)). A reward is arguably a pure benefit, but accepting terms that license filmed content, process data and limit liability is not. The Code on the Rights of the Child treats every under-18 as a child, bans providing them "information hazardous to the child", including content encouraging violence or crime or promoting gambling, and bans games or audiovisual products with such content (Arts. 3(o), 40, 66(4)); it also protects children from work harmful to health (Arts. 54 to 55) ([Code on the Rights of the Child](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)). The Advertising Law separately bars depicting minors in dangerous places and circumstances (Art. 14) ([Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)). ComCom's 2020 rules require website owners to age-tag their audiovisual content, with fines under Administrative Offences Code Art. 144¹¹ ([ComCom](https://www.comcom.ge/en/news/press-releases/gncc-to-protect-children-from-dangerous-materials-online.page)). The game's backend currently runs a 16+ registration gate, which is defensible for the free social layer, but cash tasks should be **18+ with ID verification at first withdrawal**. If 16 to 17 year olds are kept, they need verified parental consent, payouts to a parent's account, a restricted task pool and stricter moderation, and the TikTok Lite precedent shows how a regulator views rewards-for-tasks aimed at minors.

### Consumer protection and advertising: Georgian terms, no blanket waivers, labelled content

The Law on the Protection of Consumer Rights was adopted 29 March 2022 and took effect **1 June 2022**, not 2024 as one research note assumed; its enforcement chapter applied from 1 November 2022 ([Matsne 5420598](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598); [Andersen](https://ge.andersen.com/?p=4895)). Whether a non-paying player is a "consumer" of STIFF is untested, so the cautious reading treats the ToS as consumer standard terms. That has three consequences. Key information must be in Georgian (Art. 5(1)). Ambiguous terms are read for the consumer, and a term that breaches good faith and causes unjustified imbalance is invalid (Arts. 21 to 22(1)), which exposes clauses letting STIFF forfeit balances or change rewards at its discretion. And **Art. 22(3)(a) voids any term excluding liability for death or serious harm caused by the trader's gross negligence**, while Art. 22(3)(s) voids terms restricting remedies or forcing arbitration outside Georgian law. The Competition Agency's first step is an order, with fines up to 2% of the prior year's turnover (minimum GEL 600) for not complying (Art. 34) ([Matsne 5420598](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)). Practical drafting follows: objective cheat rules, an appeal path, no forfeiture of approved earnings without a specific stated breach, published reward rates, and changes applied only to future tasks with notice.

The Advertising Law requires advertising to be recognisable as such without special knowledge (Art. 4(1)), bans ads that urge "dangerous actions that may harm human health or threaten his/her safety" (Art. 4(11)), bans using a person's image without consent (Art. 4(9)), and makes the advertising customer liable for content (Art. 20) ([Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)). Player videos STIFF pays for and reposts are advertising: label them (#რეკლამა / paid by STIFF), and blur or obtain releases for bystanders. A June 2026 amendment exists but was not read ([Matsne 31840](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31840)).

### Liability and insurance: curation is both the defence and the exposure

Georgian contractual liability is fault-based (Civil Code Art. 395(1)), scholarship treats Arts. 992 to 993 as the tort base, and Art. 54 voids transactions contrary to public order or morality ([Civil Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702); [TSU Journal of Law](https://jlaw.tsu.ge/index.php/JLaw/article/download/7078/7077/11906)); the verbatim tort articles could not be retrieved and must be checked. Because STIFF designs, rewards and approves each task, a court could find a duty of care in task design and treat a reward for a risky act as a causal contribution to harm, with STIFF pleaded as joint tortfeasor when a player injures a bystander. On the criminal side, Georgian police opened a 2017 "Blue Whale" investigation under Criminal Code Art. 115 (bringing a person to suicide) within days of public alarm, later finding no confirmed presence of the game in Georgia ([MIA](https://mia.police.ge/en/shinagan-saqmeta-saministros-gantskhadeba/10662)). That episode shows how fast authorities move; a no-self-harm rule and moderation logs are the defence. Insurance offers little cover today: Ardi lists public liability for mass-gathering venues and event cancellation ([Ardi](https://ardi.ge/en/industry/39-entertainment-event-management-advertising)), GPI sells mandatory liability cover for mass-gathering facilities under a 2019 decree ([GPI](https://www.gpih.ge/en/?p=7126)), and no Georgian insurer publicly covers online dare participants. Standard public liability policies typically exclude harm participants cause themselves through voluntary risk, so a broker must place general liability for platform operations and group accident cover for any live finale. The task pool is the only real insurance.

## The safe design is a closed-loop reward ledger launched in three stages

The design that keeps all three regimes closed has a short specification. Two currencies never convert: **GEL**, earned only by approved tasks and funded only by STIFF, and **coins**, which may be bought but buy only cosmetics or brand perks and never affect a payout, a chance or task access. The GEL balance is non-transferable, cannot be topped up, and is redeemable only by withdrawal to an account in the player's verified name, described in the ToS as STIFF's accrued payable for licensed content. Rewards are fixed per task and published; escalation tracks creative effort under a hard per-task and per-day GEL ceiling; any prize that involves chance is either removed or run under a promotional-draw permit with free entry. Daily earning and time caps, a documented risk assessment of the task pool, and an always-visible report button answer the TikTok Lite and *Anderson* lessons.

Stage zero is paperwork. Commission a written opinion from Georgian counsel on the classification questions below, register with the bank for API access and signing rights, appoint an outsourced DPO, and publish Georgian-language ToS, official rules and a task-safety policy. Stage one is a closed beta: 18+, Georgia-only, web/PWA for the GEL layer, ID and IBAN-holder matching before first withdrawal, a 10 to 20 GEL minimum, weekly Bonline or BIS batches signed in the bank UI, 20% withholding computed on monthly per-person totals and filed by the 15th, and payouts marked paid only after bank status confirms. Stage two adds Flitt card withdrawals once volume justifies the token-capture flow, and a season finale with a fixed published prize; any draw-based sponsor prize waits for a permit. Store apps, if built, carry only the coin and social layers.

## Conclusion

The instinct is to treat "real money" as the regulatory problem, but in Georgia the money itself is the easy part: paying one's own debt from one's own bank account needs no licence, and the bank APIs already provide batching, idempotency and a human signing step. What actually decides the project's legal shape is a handful of product choices that look minor in a design review, such as whether a coin can ever touch a payout, whether a tie is broken by lot, or whether the balance can move between friends. Each of these is a switch between "marketing expense" and "unlicensed gambling or e-money", and the game-admin panel and backend should enforce them as invariants rather than leave them to policy.

The second shift is economic. At 0.50 GEL, tax and transfer fees can consume a third or more of the reward, so the easy tier works as an engagement token, not a wage, and the real value lies in larger creative tasks, wishlist product rewards in the novel's spirit, and content STIFF licenses for its own marketing. That framing is also what makes the tax treatment defensible. The firm conclusion is that STIFF should copy Nerve's spectacle and none of its leverage, because Georgian law already reflects the film's moral: the operator who sets the dare owns the consequences.
