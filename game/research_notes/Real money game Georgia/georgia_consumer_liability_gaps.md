# Georgia: consumer protection, dare liability, payment-law exemptions, minors and insurance (gaps for STIFF's reward game, as of Nov 2026)

Scope: a Georgian LLC (STIFF) runs a free-to-enter online dare/task game. It pays fixed GEL rewards per approved task and holds earnings in a balance until withdrawal. Sources were read directly where possible. Where the official text could not be retrieved (notably the Civil Code tort articles), that is stated under Gaps. Nothing in these notes is legal advice.

## 1. Consumer protection: Law of Georgia on the Protection of Consumer Rights

### Takeaway
The brief's premise is slightly off. The Law was adopted on 29 March 2022 and mostly took effect on **1 June 2022**, not 2024. Its enforcement chapter (Arts. 28-36) took effect on 1 November 2022. Online terms of service fall under its Georgian-language duty (Art. 5(1)), its distance-contract rules (Arts. 10-15) and its unfair-terms rules (Arts. 21-22). The Agency enforces it and can fine up to 2% of annual turnover, with a GEL 600 minimum, for not complying with its decisions (Art. 34).

### Cited Findings
**Status and timing (settled law)**
- Parliament adopted the Law on 29 March 2022. Its main provisions took effect on 1 June 2022 — [Andersen Georgia](https://ge.andersen.com/?p=4895); [matsne, Law on the Protection of Consumer Rights](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 39(1): the Law took effect on 1 June 2022. Art. 39(2): Arts. 28, 29(2)-(4) and 30-36 (complaints, the Agency's powers, investigations, fines and appeals) apply from 1 November 2022 — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- One academic source says the Law largely mirrors the EU consumer-rights acquis (the Consumer Rights Directive) — [UNCTAD / Andersen summaries](https://ge.andersen.com/?p=4895)

**Digital content (settled law)**
- Art. 4(p) defines "digital content" as information produced and provided in digital form. Art. 4(l) counts goods "disseminated in a digital form" as goods. Art. 5(1)(i) requires disclosure of the functionality and compatibility of digital content. The English text retrieved has **no separate definition of "digital service"** — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)

**Language (settled law)**
- Art. 5(1): pre-contract information must be given "in the national language of Georgia," clearly and understandably. Art. 11(1) sets the same rule for off-premises contracts. Art. 26(1)(v) treats offering after-sales service in another language, without first telling the consumer in Georgian, as a potentially misleading practice — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- A secondary source agrees: information must always be provided in Georgian, and a foreign language may be added — [expathub.ge](https://expathub.ge/consumer-protection-in-georgia-know-your-rights/)

**Pre-contract information and distance contracts (settled law)**
- Art. 5(1)(a)-(j) lists the required disclosures: trader identity and contacts, characteristics, full price, payment and delivery terms, contract term, and withdrawal conditions. Under Art. 5(6), the consumer need not pay undisclosed extra charges — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 4(d) defines a distance contract. Art. 10(1)(a)-(i) adds information duties for distance contracts. **Art. 10(3): Art. 10 does not apply to goods or services priced at or below GEL 30** — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 12(2): for electronic orders, the consumer must expressly acknowledge the payment obligation, or is not bound to pay. Art. 12(6): the trader must confirm the contract on a durable medium — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 13(1): consumers have 14 days to withdraw. Art. 13(8)-(9): if the trader gave no withdrawal information, the period extends up to 12 months. Art. 14(2)(l): there is no withdrawal right for digital content on a tangible medium once performance has begun with the consumer's consent and acknowledgement — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)

**Unfair contract terms (settled law)**
- Art. 21: standard terms bind only if the consumer could know them. Ambiguous terms are read in the consumer's favour — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 22(1), the general clause: a term is invalid if it breaches good faith and causes an unjustified imbalance to the consumer's detriment. Art. 22(2) excludes the adequacy of the price from that test — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 22(3)(a)-(s) is a list of void terms. It includes **(a) excluding or limiting liability for death or serious harm to health caused by the trader's gross negligence** and **(s) restricting the consumer's legal remedies or forcing arbitration outside Georgian law**. Art. 22(4): one invalid term does not void the rest. No separate "grey list" was found — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)

**Enforcement body and penalties (settled law)**
- The Georgian National Competition Agency is the authority under the Law (it uses the gcca.gov.ge domain and the brief calls it the Competition and Consumer Protection Agency) — [Andersen Georgia](https://ge.andersen.com/?p=4895); [bm.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/the-national-competition-agency-approved-the-rules-for-calculating-fines-in-case-of-violation-of-consumer-rights)
- Art. 29(1)-(2): the Agency shares enforcement with sector regulators and does not cover banking, insurance or energy. **Inference:** payment-service consumer complaints go to the NBG — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- Art. 29(4)(a)-(n) lists the Agency's powers: examining collective violations, requesting information, going to court, setting deadlines to restore rights, and **(i) prohibiting unfair standard terms**. Under Art. 32(8) it can ask a court to suspend a trader's conduct. Art. 33(8)-(10) covers binding commitments. Art. 33(7): decisions are published. Art. 35: decisions can be appealed to court within 30 days — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- **Art. 34(1): failing to comply with an Agency decision is fined up to 2% of the prior year's turnover, with a GEL 600 minimum.** Art. 34(2) sets the method for new traders. Art. 34(4): a repeat within 12 months doubles the fine. Art. 34(4¹): failing to give the Agency information costs GEL 500, or GEL 1,000 for a repeat. Art. 34(6): fines are payable within one month — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)
- The Agency has adopted rules for calculating these fines — [bm.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/the-national-competition-agency-approved-the-rules-for-calculating-fines-in-case-of-violation-of-consumer-rights)
- The fine only follows a breached Agency decision. The first step is a decision ordering the trader to stop, not a fine. A secondary source says the same — [madloba.info](https://madloba.info/en/blog/laws-of-georgia/zakon-o-zashchite-prav-potrebiteley-v-gruzii-v-2022-godu) (consistent with Art. 34 text on matsne)
- A separate e-commerce regime was introduced in 2023 — [KPMG Georgia](https://kpmg.com/ge/en/home/insights/2023/07/electronic-commerce.html)

### Inferences
- **Is a player a "consumer," and STIFF a "trader," in the reward relationship?** This is interpretation, not settled law. The player pays nothing and STIFF pays the player. The Law is built around a consumer acquiring goods or services. Two readings are possible. On one, the game account (a service supplied in exchange for the player's content and data) is a consumer contract. On the other, the paid-task relationship is closer to a service or work contract in which the player is the supplier. A cautious operator should assume the ToS are consumer standard terms, so that Arts. 5, 21 and 22 apply to the platform-use relationship. No source addresses free-to-enter, user-paid models.
- **Georgian-language ToS:** Art. 5(1) applies to pre-contract information. The ToS and key information (rewards, approval rules, withdrawal of balance, fees) should be in Georgian, with English optional. Under Art. 21, terms the consumer could not understand risk not binding. This is interpretation, but low-risk advice.
- **Liability waivers in the ToS:** Art. 22(3)(a) voids any attempt to exclude liability for death or serious injury caused by STIFF's gross negligence. A blanket "players participate at their own risk; STIFF is not liable for any injury" clause is therefore at least partly void if STIFF is a trader and the player a consumer. Clauses such as "STIFF may withhold or forfeit the balance at its discretion" or "unilaterally change rewards" are exposed under the Art. 22(1) imbalance test. This applies the statute; no Agency case law was found.
- Arts. 10-13 (distance contracts and withdrawal) matter little for a free service. Art. 10(3), the GEL 30 floor, likely removes the extra distance-contract information duties for zero-price use.

### Gaps
- No Agency decisions or published guidance on online ToS, gaming or user-reward platforms were found.
- The exact wording of each item in the Art. 22(3) list beyond (a) and (s) was not captured. It should be read in full on matsne before drafting the ToS.
- The fine-calculation rules themselves (number and date) were not retrieved.
- It is unverified whether the Law was amended in 2024-2026. The matsne consolidated-version list was not checked for this Law.

## 2. Liability for harm during dares: Civil Code and Criminal Code

### Takeaway
The official English Civil Code text of Arts. 992-1012 could **not** be retrieved: matsne returned truncated pages and a PHP error on the PDF. Fault-based liability is confirmed (Art. 395(1) for contract), and Georgian scholarship treats Arts. 992-993 as the tort base. Consumer-law Art. 22(3)(a) independently voids waivers for gross-negligence death or serious injury. On the criminal side, Art. 115 (bringing a person to suicide) is the confirmed article. Georgian police used it, with Art. 285, in the 2017 "Blue Whale" investigation, which ended with no confirmed presence of the game in Georgia. No dedicated anti-"challenge" law was found.

### Cited Findings
**Civil**
- Art. 54 of the Civil Code: a transaction that breaches legal prohibitions, public order or morality is void. Art. 10(3): acts contrary to mandatory norms are void. Art. 115: rights may not be exercised solely to harm another — [matsne, Civil Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- Art. 395(1): an obligor is liable only for damage caused intentionally or negligently (fault-based contractual liability). Art. 396: liability for representatives and employees — [matsne, Civil Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- Tbilisi State University scholarship treats Arts. 992-993 as the tort provisions protecting personal rights, and discusses non-pecuniary damage under them. Another TSU article cites Art. 992 alongside Art. 395(1) in discussing the fault principle and departures from it — [TSU Journal of Law](https://jlaw.tsu.ge/index.php/JLaw/article/download/7078/7077/11906); [TSU Journal of Law](https://jlaw.tsu.ge/index.php/JLaw/article/download/2379/2532/3048)
- Consumer-law Art. 22(3)(a): a term excluding liability for death or serious harm caused by gross negligence is void — [matsne, Consumer Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598)

**Criminal and news**
- In 2017 the Ministry of Internal Affairs (Central Criminal Police) opened an investigation into "Blue Whale" under **Art. 115 (bringing a person to suicide)** and **Art. 285 (creating or spreading malicious computer programs)**. The aim was to see whether the game was linked to Georgian internet space — [MIA statement](https://mia.police.ge/en/shinagan-saqmeta-saministros-gantskhadeba/10662); [The Messenger, 16 Oct 2017](https://messenger.com.ge/issues/3994_october_16_2017/3994_tea.html)
- The Ministry later stated that, on the materials available, the real existence of Blue Whale in Georgian online space was not confirmed — [MIA statement](https://mia.police.ge/en/shinagan-saqmeta-saministros-gantskhadeba/10662)
- Art. 115 is used in practice outside online contexts, for example in domestic-violence cases (the 14-year-old "Nini" case, a GYLA case sent to the ECtHR). Commentators say the difficulty is proving causation and qualifying the crime — [femicide.ge](https://www.femicide.ge/eng/list/show/291-The-Incitement-to-Suicide-of-14-year-old-Nini--flaws-in-Georgian-legislation-and-execution); [GYLA](https://gyla.ge/en/post/qalis-tvitmkvlelobamde-miyvanis-saqme-evropul-sasamartloshi-gaigzavna); [Journal of Law and European Studies 2023](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/jles-2023-0010)
- Criminal Code text: [matsne, Criminal Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/16426?publication=273) (not parsed article by article in this session)

### Inferences
- **Tort exposure (interpretation):** STIFF designs, publishes, rewards and approves the tasks. A court could find a duty of care in task design and moderation, and treat a reward for a risky act as a causal contribution to the harm. Under a fault-based standard, the defensive levers are documented risk review of the task pool, refusing dangerous submissions, and not rewarding risky footage. If a dare harms a third party (a passer-by, property), the player is the primary tortfeasor. STIFF could still be pleaded as a joint tortfeasor or instigator.
- **Waivers (interpretation):** Under consumer-law Art. 22(3)(a), a waiver cannot cover gross negligence causing death or serious injury. Civil Code Art. 54 (public order and morality) is a further basis to void a waiver of liability for harm to health. A waiver covering ordinary negligence and minor harm may survive, but its value is limited. Risk acknowledgements (assumption of risk, contributory fault) are likely more useful than exclusions.
- **Criminal (interpretation):** Art. 115 needs a link to suicide, so it is unlikely for ordinary dares. It becomes relevant if self-harm tasks appear or a player is pressured, which is the Blue Whale pattern. The 2017 episode shows the authorities will open an investigation fast on public alarm. Moderation logs and a no-self-harm rule are the practical defence.

### Gaps
- **The verbatim text of Civil Code Arts. 992, 993, 997 (employer liability), 998 (joint liability), 999-1000 (increased danger), 1005-1008, 413 (non-pecuniary damage) and 415 (contributory fault) was not retrieved.** Matsne's English page truncated at about 100k characters and the PDF errored. The usual numbering is that Art. 992 is the general tort clause (unlawful, intentional or negligent harm), Art. 998 covers joint liability, Art. 413 non-pecuniary damage and Art. 415 contributory fault. This is from background knowledge only and **must be verified** on the Georgian text at matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/31702.
- Whether Civil Code Art. 395(2), or another article, prohibits excluding liability for intent in advance was not verified.
- No Criminal Code articles specific to inciting dangerous acts or "challenges" were confirmed. Candidates such as involving minors in antisocial acts, endangering health, and the Art. 117-120 health-harm articles were not checked.
- No Georgian legislative response to Blue Whale (2017-2023), and no later Georgian news on TikTok-style dangerous challenges, was found.

## 3. Law on Payment Systems and Payment Services: current text, exemptions, NBG registration

### Takeaway
The Law is current to amendment No. 1890-VIმს-XIმპ of 15 October 2026. That amendment and the Dec 2025 one did **not** touch e-money or the exclusions: the Oct 2026 law changes Art. 18 (safeguarding customer funds) and Art. 41⁹ (appeals). The closest thing to a PSD2 limited-network exclusion is **Art. 13(2)'s carve-out for instruments usable only in or on the issuer's premises**. There is no express loyalty or limited-network exclusion, but **Art. 13(3) lets the NBG define more exclusions**. The NBG's capital rules for PSPs could not be confirmed from primary text.

### Cited Findings
- The latest consolidated version on matsne is dated 15/10/2026. The latest amendment is Law No. 1890-VIმს-XIმპ (adopted 15/10/2026, published 16/10/2026). The history runs back to 2013 — [matsne, Payment Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- Law 1890-VIმს-XIმპ takes effect on publication. It replaces Art. 18(2): customer funds must sit in nominal holding accounts, separate from own funds. New Art. 18(2¹) lets a registered PSP that is a significant-system participant place customer funds at the NBG, on NBG conditions. New Art. 18(2²) bars using customer funds as security for the PSP's obligations. Art. 41⁹(8): appeals within one month to Tbilisi City Court, with no suspensive effect. **It does not mention e-money, exclusions, registration or capital** — [matsne, Law 1890](https://matsne.gov.ge/document/view/7023423)
- An earlier amendment, Law 1303-IVმს-XIმპ of 17/12/2025, took effect on 2 March 2026. Its content was not examined — [matsne search listing](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/1673253)
- A September 2025 draft would let the NBG cap fees for systemically important systems. Its adoption was not confirmed — [EY Georgia tax brief Q3 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_ge/technical/tax-alerts/2025/10/ey-georgia-tax-law-brief-for-july-september-2025)
- Virtual asset service providers were brought under full NBG supervision by amendments to the Organic Law on the NBG and the Payment Law — [NBG news](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/the-national-bank-of-georgia-expands-the-supervisory-framework-for-virtual-asset-service-p)
- Art. 2 definitions: "electronic money" is the electronically stored nominal value of funds received from users to execute payment transactions, **accepted as a means of payment by persons other than the issuer**. An "electronic money provider" is a PSP that issues e-money. The definition was changed by Law No. 5681 of 20 Dec 2019 — [matsne, Payment Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253); [NBG English PDF](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/law-on-payment-services)
- Art. 13(2) lists what is not a payment service: commercial-agent payments; **instruments usable only in or on the issuer's premises**; technical or IT providers that never possess funds (item f); certain telecom and digital payments (item h, with an NBG cap described as GEL 300 per month per subscriber for carrier billing); cash payments; intra-system and intra-group transfers; and cheques. **There is no express loyalty or limited-network (PSD2 Art. 3(k)) exclusion. Art. 13(3) lets the NBG set further exclusions** — [matsne, Payment Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253); [NBG Payment Services/Instruments](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/payment-services-instruments)
- Art. 15: only NBG-registered PSPs, commercial banks, microfinance organisations, or persons under Art. 14(1) may provide payment services. The NBG registers under its own procedure (Art. 15(4)) and publishes a register (Art. 15(5)) — [matsne, Payment Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- The Law sets no capital amount. Significant PSPs must meet minimum capital set by the NBG (Arts. 17(3), 44(2)). Under Art. 44(1), documents are due within 30 days of being designated significant. Under Art. 17(9), audited statements are due by 15 May. Art. 51 gave pre-existing businesses 3 months after commencement (the Law has been in force since 1 July 2012, Art. 52) — [matsne, Payment Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- The NBG regulation on capital adequacy for PSPs took effect on 1 Jan 2024. It sets minimum regulatory capital for **significant** PSPs and for payment-initiation providers. The amounts were not retrieved — [NBG news](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/nbg-approves-regulation-on-capital-adequacy-requirements-for-payment-service-providers)
- Secondary sources conflict on entry capital and fees. A vendor (2026) says there is no minimum capital and a GEL 5,000 fee. A regulatory guide says GEL 50,000 entry capital. An NBG page cited in search snippets gives a registration fee of GEL 10,000. **These are unresolved** — [zitadelleag.com](https://www.zitadelleag.com/services/payment-licensing/georgia-psp); [regalert.today](https://regalert.today/guides/fintech/GE); [NBG PSP page](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/payment-service-providers-2)
- A 2021 NBG regulation on card-based instruments, including e-money instruments, applies together with the Law — [NBG PDF](https://nbg.gov.ge/fm/ინდივიდუალურ_სამართლებრივი_აქტები/ნორმატიული_აქტები/საგადახდო/card-based-instrument-regulation-en-state-155-04-28-02-2021.pdf)
- In 2026 the NBG tightened payment-service rules: protections for customers over 60 (Order 87/04 of 8 Apr 2026, effective 1 Sep 2026) and a 48-hour suspension for high-risk transactions — [NBG news](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/national-bank-of-georgia-tightens-payment-service-requirements-to-protect-customers-aged-6); [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/nbg-introduces-48-hour-suspension-for-high-risk-transactions/)
- The 2023 overhaul of PSP regulation — [KPMG Georgia](https://kpmg.com/ge/en/home/insights/2023/06/payment-service-providers.html)

### Inferences
- **Is STIFF's balance e-money? (interpretation)** The Art. 2 definition rests on (i) funds *received from users* and (ii) acceptance by persons *other than the issuer*. STIFF's balance is funded by STIFF, not by users, and is redeemable only by STIFF, either as a payout or for coins and shop items. On a plain reading it fails both limbs. It is therefore better characterised as **a debt STIFF owes the player under the task contract** (accrued remuneration) than as e-money or a payment account. Its legal nature is a contract or civil-law claim, not a payment instrument.
- **This changes if players can top up the balance or transfer it peer to peer, or if third parties accept it.** Any of those moves it toward e-money or a payment service, and the narrow "issuer's premises" exclusion plus NBG discretion under Art. 13(3) would then be the only fallback. A **closed-loop balance funded only by the operator, with no top-up and no P2P transfer**, is the defensible design.
- The payout itself (GEL to a player's bank account or card) should run through a bank or a registered PSP, so STIFF is not executing payment transactions for others. STIFF pays its own debt from its own account, which is not a payment service.
- The new Art. 18 safeguarding rules (Oct 2026) apply only to PSPs. They are a useful voluntary benchmark: keep player balances in a segregated account.

### Gaps
- The full text of Art. 13(2) items (a)-(k) in the 15/10/2026 version, especially the exact "issuer's premises" wording and whether any NBG order under Art. 13(3) adds a limited-network or loyalty exclusion, was not read directly.
- The content of Law 1303-IVმს-XIმპ (17 Dec 2025) was not checked.
- The current NBG order on PSP registration (number, required documents, review deadline) and the minimum-capital figure for non-significant PSPs and e-money providers were not confirmed from primary text. The secondary figures conflict.
- No NBG guidance or law-firm brief on reward points, game balances or loyalty schemes was found.

## 4. Code on the Rights of the Child (2019) and ComCom rules on minors online

### Takeaway
The Code treats every under-18 as a child (Art. 3(a)). It bans giving children "information hazardous to the child" (Art. 40(1)), which covers content encouraging violence or crime and promoting alcohol, tobacco, drugs or **gambling** (Art. 3(o)). It also bans providing games or audiovisual products with such content (Art. 66(4)). ComCom's 2020 online rules require website owners to age-label their sites, with fines under Administrative Offences Code Art. 144¹¹. Nothing found specifically regulates dares or challenges, or advertising to 16-17 year olds.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 3(a): a child is a minor under 18. The latest consolidated version seen was 26/06/2025 (Law No 880 amended Art. 26) — [matsne, Code on the Rights of the Child](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)
- Art. 3(o): "information hazardous to the child" includes encouraging violence or crime, sexual content, promoting alcohol, tobacco, drugs or gambling, and detailed scenes of violence or cruelty. A later version adds content on gender identity. The English versions differ, so check the current text — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854); [CoE English translation](https://rm.coe.int/georgia-law-of-georgia-the-code-on-the-rights-of-the-child/1680ad3cb5)
- Art. 40(1) bans providing such information to a child. Art. 40(2): non-compliance entails liability under Georgian law — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)
- Art. 66: broadcasters must protect children from harmful information (66(1)). Selling or providing harmful print media (66(3)), or **movies, games or audiovisual products with harmful content** (66(4)), to children is banned. 66(7) is an internet-café night curfew. 66(8) was truncated in the fetch — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)
- Art. 14(3) gives children the right to access media and the internet. Art. 15(4)(d): the State must develop guidance on protecting children from harmful material — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)
- Arts. 54-55: protection from harmful work and economic exploitation. Art. 54(2) requires the State to list hazardous work. Art. 55(3): no work that harms health or education. Art. 19(2): a child's transactions depend on age and require a legal representative — [matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4613854)
- ComCom adopted rules on publishing material dangerous to children and youth (announced 4 March 2020, in force 1 June 2020). ISPs must offer a parental blocking mechanism and update ComCom's harmful-sites list weekly. **Website owners must attach an age tag** for their audiovisual content. ComCom compiles the harmful-sites list and randomly monitors websites. **Breaches are fined under Art. 144¹¹ of the Administrative Offences Code** (amount not stated) — [ComCom press release](https://www.comcom.ge/en/news/press-releases/gncc-to-protect-children-from-dangerous-materials-online.page); [agenda.ge](https://agenda.ge/en/news/2020/665); [CoE Tbilisi](https://www.coe.int/en/web/tbilisi/supporting-freedom-of-media-and-internet-in-georgia/-/asset_publisher/44hdTgZXMRnc/content/resolution-on-child-online-safety-supported-by-the-council-of-europe-entered-into-force)
- ComCom fines broadcasters (e.g. GEL 2,500 each to Rustavi 2 and GDS TV) for programmes unsuitable for minors under the Law on Broadcasting — [ComCom](https://comcom.ge/en/news/press-releases/communications-commission-issues-rustavi-2-and-gds-tv-with-fines-of-gel-2500-each-for-broadcasting-programmes-unsuitable-for-minors.page)
- 2017 amendments to the Law on Broadcasting gave ComCom a media-literacy mandate — [CoE](https://www.coe.int/en/web/freedom-expression/-/media-literacy-initiatives-engage-students-teachers-and-academic-institutions)

### Inferences
- **16-17 year olds (interpretation):** Dares that encourage violence, crime or risky consumption, and user-generated videos of them shown to minors, plausibly fall within Art. 3(o) and Art. 40(1) and 66(4) ("games ... with harmful content"). The Code has no general ban on dares, but a dare game open to minors that shows violent or law-breaking content runs against it. Rewarding minors for physically risky acts is also arguably "work" or economic activity that harms health (Arts. 54-55), which is a stretch but an argument a regulator could make.
- **Gambling crossover:** if any coin-shop or random-reward mechanic looks like gambling, Art. 3(o) bans promoting it to children. This interacts with the separate gambling-law notes.
- **Contract capacity:** Art. 19(2) plus Civil Code capacity rules mean a 16-17 year old's acceptance of the ToS and of payouts likely needs legal-representative consent. This is interpretation; the Civil Code capacity articles were not verified.
- The ComCom age tag applies to the website. STIFF's site should carry an age label (e.g. 18+, or a 16+ variant if minors are admitted).

### Gaps
- Art. 66(8) and anything after it (possibly website duties) was not read because of fetch truncation.
- The ComCom resolution number, full text, and the fine amount under AOC Art. 144¹¹ were not retrieved.
- No provision found on advertising to minors. The Law on Advertising was not checked.
- No Georgian rule specific to "challenge" content was found.

## 5. Insurance: event and participant accident cover, public liability

### Takeaway
Ardi publicly lists public liability insurance for places of mass gathering and event-cancellation products for the event industry. GPI markets mandatory civil liability cover for mass-gathering facilities under a 27 March 2019 government decree. No public page was found for an Aldagi, GPI, Imedi L or Ardi product covering participants of an online dare game. Cover would need a bespoke broker placement.

### Cited Findings
- Ardi's entertainment, event-management and advertising page lists: public liability insurance for places of mass gathering, employee health insurance, installations and structures insurance, car insurance, event cancellation/postponement, and ticket cancellation/postponement. No participant accident cover and no limits are listed. The page refers enquiries to a corporate group leader — [Ardi](https://ardi.ge/en/industry/39-entertainment-event-management-advertising)
- GPI says civil liability insurance is mandatory for mass-gathering facilities (shopping centres, markets, gas stations, hotels) under the government decree of 27 March 2019 — [GPI Holding](https://www.gpih.ge/en/?p=7126)

### Inferences
- **Mandatory cover (interpretation):** the 2019 mass-gathering decree targets physical venues. It does not apply to an online game unless STIFF runs physical live events.
- **Fit (interpretation):** typical public liability policies, the ones STIFF could plausibly buy, cover third-party injury or damage arising from the insured's premises or activities. They usually exclude harm the insured's "participants" cause themselves while voluntarily doing risky acts. Group personal-accident cover for named participants is a standard product line for Georgian insurers, but nothing public confirms it for online players. A broker should be asked for (a) general/public liability covering platform operations and (b) a group accident policy for live-event participants.

### Gaps
- No public product information was found for Aldagi or Imedi L liability or accident lines in this session.
- No premiums, limits or exclusions for any Georgian insurer were found.
- Whether any Georgian insurer will underwrite a dare/challenge platform is unknown. It needs a direct broker enquiry.
