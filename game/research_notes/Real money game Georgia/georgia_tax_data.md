# Georgian tax, accounting, personal-data and advertising obligations for STIFF's paid-task game

Scope: a Georgian LLC (STIFF) paying many individuals 0.50 GEL to a few hundred GEL for completing tasks in an online game, and collecting videos, photos, ID documents and bank details. Jurisdiction: Georgia, as of November 2026.

**Source caveat (applies throughout).** matsne.gov.ge blocked bulk download, so the Tax Code article text below was read from an English consolidated translation of the Tax Code dated 2023 (a copy of the matsne text, carrying matsne page footers), hosted at [alservice.ge](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf). matsne lists the latest consolidated Tax Code as publication 248, dated 28/10/2026 ([matsne, Tax Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1043717)). Any amendment made between mid-2023 and October 2026 to the articles quoted here has **not** been checked. The Personal Data Protection Law was read on matsne, but only in its **initial (2023) version**. matsne lists later consolidated versions up to 10/06/2026 ([matsne, PDP Law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)). Re-verify every article number against the current consolidated text before relying on it.

## 1. Tax on rewards: classification, withholding, deductibility, VAT, non-residents

### Takeaway
Georgian law has no general "prize" exemption that fits a skill or task reward from a company. Under the Tax Code, a payment to an individual who is not a registered entrepreneur is taxed at 20% PIT, withheld at source. That holds whether the payment is treated as payment for services (Art. 154(1)(d)) or as winnings from a promotional game (Art. 154(1)(e)). The withholding must be remitted when paid and reported by the 15th of the following month, with each recipient's registration (personal ID) number. If the reward is framed as payment for content or services, it is an ordinary business expense with no profit tax. If it is framed as a gratuitous transfer, it becomes a 15% distributed-profit-tax object. Classification is the main decision.

### Cited Findings
**Rates**
- Art. 81(1): "A natural person's taxable income shall be taxed at the rate of 20%, unless otherwise provided for by this Code." The reduced rates are 5% for residential rent and some capital gains, 10% for organising online games of chance, and 2% on sums withdrawn from an online gambling account under Art. 81(3^2). None of these fits task rewards. — [Tax Code (2023 English consolidated)](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Art. 98(1): profit tax rate is 15%. Art. 97(10): the taxable amount is the disbursement **divided by 0.85**, an effective 17.65% on the net amount paid out. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**Withholding agents (Art. 154(1)). A legal person withholds when:**
- (d) it "pays the cost of rendered services to a natural person ... who is not registered as an individual entrepreneur". Exceptions: VAT-registered individuals, persons with micro-business status, fixed-tax payers, notaries and enforcement officers, each in the part of their activities. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- (e) it is "a person organising a promotional game ... lotto, bingo, lottery who pays winnings to a natural person". — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- (l) it is "a person transferring property free of charge to a natural person that is not registered as an individual entrepreneur, except when this person transfers free of charge to the same natural person property with a value of up to GEL 1 000 during a tax year". In that case the procedure is set by the Minister of Finance. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**Exemptions checked (Art. 82(1))**
- (h) Gifts up to GEL 1,000 per tax year are exempt only when received "**from a natural person**", and gifts from an employer to an employee are excluded. A company's gift does not qualify. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- (w) "income received from lottery, the value of which does not exceed GEL 1 000" is exempt. This covers lotteries, not task rewards or promotional games. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- (c) The only prize exemption covers sports awards: Olympic Games, World and European championships, and similar. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- No exemption was found for promotional or marketing prizes, and none for small-amount rewards from companies. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**Mechanics: remittance, reporting, ID numbers (Art. 154(2)–(4))**
- The payer is responsible for withholding. If it fails to withhold, it pays the tax itself, with penalties (Art. 154(2)(a)–(b)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- The tax is transferred to the budget "upon paying an amount to a person" (Art. 154(3)(a)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- The agent must submit a notice "indicating the person's **registration number**, name and surname, residential address, the total amount of income and the total amount of tax withheld". The deadline is "the 15th day of the month following the month in which the tax is withheld" (Art. 154(3)(c)). A disbursement return is due by the 15th of the month after each reporting period (Art. 154(4)). The Minister may set other timeframes (Art. 154(3^1)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**Profit tax under the Estonian model (Art. 97(1))**
- The objects of profit tax for a resident enterprise are: (a) distributed profit; (b) "expenses borne or other payments not related to the economic activity"; (c) "free delivery of goods/services and/or transfer of funds"; and (d) representation expenses above the statutory limit. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**VAT**
- Money is not "goods" for VAT (Art. 160(2)), so a cash payout is not a supply by STIFF. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- A free supply of goods is deemed a supply for consideration where input VAT was deducted (Art. 160(3)(a)). Samples and a "gift of little value" are excluded. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- A "gift of little value" is a free transfer worth no more than GEL 50 excluding VAT per calendar year per natural person. "Advertisement and entertainment expenses shall not be considered a gift of little value." — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Services free of charge "for the purpose different from the one of its own activity" are deemed supplies for consideration (Art. 160^1(2)(a)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Mandatory VAT registration applies at GEL 100,000 of taxable turnover in any 12 consecutive months. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Reverse-charge VAT applies to services provided "to a tax agent in the territory of Georgia" by a supplier not established in Georgia (Art. 161(1)(a)). Any Georgian-established person other than a non-entrepreneur individual or a FIZ enterprise counts as a tax agent here. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

**Non-residents**
- Georgian-source income from services includes services "actually rendered in Georgia" (Art. 104(1)(c.a)), among other connecting rules. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- A non-resident's Georgian-source income not attributable to a Georgian permanent establishment is taxed at source without deductions (Art. 134(1)). Rates: salary at the Art. 81 rate; "other paid amounts ... regarded as Georgian source income" at **10%** (134(1)(e)); **15%** for persons registered in preferential-tax jurisdictions for (b), (b1) and (e) (134(1^1)). A non-resident may file by 1 April to have the withheld tax recalculated (134(3)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- PwC's summary for non-resident enterprises agrees: income from services rendered in Georgia, 10%; other Georgian-source income, 10%; "black listed" countries, 15%; domestic rates apply where a treaty is less favourable. — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/georgia/corporate/withholding-taxes)

### Inferences
- **Most defensible classification: payment for services.** The player films a video or completes a task, and STIFF gets a deliverable it uses for marketing. That fits Art. 154(1)(d): withhold 20%, and the cost is an economic-activity expense, so there is no Art. 97 profit tax. This depends on documenting a commercial purpose, such as a licence over the content and marketing use.
- **"Promotional game" (154(1)(e)) probably does not apply.** The Law on Lotteries defines a promotional game by a chance-based draw (see Q6 findings). A skill or task reward without chance falls outside that law (Art. 4). Either way, the PIT rate is still 20%.
- **"Gift" framing is worse.** Below GEL 1,000 per person per year, Art. 154(1)(l) lifts STIFF's withholding duty, but the exemption in Art. 82(1)(h) does not cover gifts from a company. The recipient therefore probably remains liable to self-declare, under a procedure set by Ministerial order that was not reviewed here. STIFF would also owe profit tax on the free transfer under Art. 97(1)(c), at an effective 17.65%. Avoid this framing.
- **Registered entrepreneurs** (individual entrepreneurs, small-business 1% status holders, micro-business status holders) are partly outside 154(1)(d). An individual entrepreneur with small-business status could invoice and pay their own tax, which suits high-volume creators. Confirm each recipient's status before relying on it.
- **VAT on cash rewards:** none. **Coin-shop rewards paid in merchandise** are a free supply of goods, VAT-able if STIFF deducted input VAT on them. The GEL 50 "gift of little value" carve-out probably fails, because advertising expenses are excluded from it. Such rewards are also a potential Art. 97(1)(c) profit-tax object unless they are treated as consideration for the content. This is interpretation.
- **Foreign hosting** (Supabase, Fly.io, Cloudflare): STIFF, as a Georgian-established person, probably owes reverse-charge VAT under Art. 161 on these services. This is independent of the game.
- **Non-resident players** filming outside Georgia: under Art. 104(1)(c.a) the service is probably not Georgian-source, which would mean no Georgian withholding. Treaty and residency (the 183-day test) still need checking. If they perform in Georgia, 10% under 134(1)(e) or 20% may apply, depending on whether the payment is treated as service income or salary-like. This is unsettled.

### Gaps
- The October 2026 consolidated text of Arts. 81, 82, 97, 134 and 154 was not read. Rates or thresholds may have changed since the 2023 copy.
- No rs.ge (Revenue Service) guidance or infohub ruling was found specifically on rewards for user-generated content or task completion.
- The current rs.ge e-declaration form names were not verified. They are commonly known as the monthly "withholding (გადახდის წყაროსთან დაკავებული გადასახადის) declaration" and the disbursement return.
- The Ministerial order under Art. 154(1)(l) on gifts under GEL 1,000 was not reviewed.
- Funded (cumulative) pension contributions (2% + 2%) were not verified. Whether they apply to service-contract payments to individuals is unconfirmed, and they likely do not apply to non-salary income.

## 2. Gross-up options: worked examples

### Takeaway
At a 20% PIT rate, paying the advertised amount **net** costs STIFF 1.25 times the advertised amount. Paying it **gross** means the player receives 80% of it. The worked examples below use only Art. 81(1) (20%) and Art. 97(10) (division by 0.85).

### Cited Findings
- PIT rate is 20% (Art. 81(1)), and the payer may bear the tax: the recipient "may pay the taxes ... on behalf of the payer" (Art. 154(2)(c)). Net-of-tax agreements are a standard gross-up. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Profit tax base equals the disbursement divided by 0.85 (Art. 97(10)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)

Worked examples (arithmetic derived from the cited rates):

| Scenario | Advertised | Gross (taxable) | PIT 20% withheld | Player receives | STIFF total cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Gross: advertised amount is gross | 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.10 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
| A. Gross | 100.00 | 100.00 | 20.00 | 80.00 | 100.00 |
| B. Net: STIFF bears tax (gross = net / 0.8) | 0.50 | 0.625, about 0.63 | 0.125, about 0.13 | 0.50 | about 0.63 |
| B. Net | 100.00 | 125.00 | 25.00 | 100.00 | 125.00 |
| C. Wrong framing: gift from company under GEL 1,000, profit tax under Art. 97(1)(c) | 100.00 | n/a | 0 withheld by STIFF (recipient may owe 20% personally) | 100.00 | 100 + 17.65 = 117.65 |

### Inferences
- Rounding to the tetri matters at 0.50 GEL. With rounding per payout, a 0.625 gross becomes 0.63 or 0.62. Computing tax on the monthly aggregate per person avoids drift, because Art. 154(3)(c) reports totals per person per month.
- In scenario B, the tax STIFF bears is itself part of the recipient's income, so the gross-up formula must be used. Adding 20% on top would under-withhold: net 100 plus 20 gives gross 120, and 20% of 120 is 24, not 20.
- The UI should say which basis applies, for example "100 GEL, tax paid by STIFF" or "100 GEL before 20% tax". The Law on Advertising bans "unreliable" claims about price and terms (Art. 3(4)).

### Gaps
- No official rs.ge example of a gross-up calculation for non-salary payments was found.

## 3. Accounting: documenting thousands of micro-payouts

### Takeaway
A clickwrap participation and content-licence agreement, accepted online and linked to the player's verified identity and personal number, plus a per-payout ledger, is the practical basis. The Tax Code requires per-person monthly totals with the registration number and address. The underlying records therefore have to support that report.

### Cited Findings
- The monthly notice must state per person the "registration number, name and surname, residential address, the total amount of income and the total amount of tax withheld" (Art. 154(3)(c)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- The tax must be remitted on payment (Art. 154(3)(a)). If it is not withheld, the payer owes it with penalties (Art. 154(2)(b)). — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- Free supplies are included in gross income at market price (Art. 102(2)). The exception is free advertising goods "which have no independent consumer specifications and are an integral part of the supply of the primary goods/services". — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf)
- The PDP Law requires written or electronic records of processing: purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention and security. These must be produced to the Service within 3 working days of a request (Art. 28(1), 28(3)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

### Inferences
- Recommended evidence pack per payout:
  - user ID linked to the verified personal number;
  - the version of the terms accepted, with timestamp and IP;
  - task ID and the content accepted by STIFF (the deliverable);
  - gross, PIT and net amounts;
  - bank transfer reference.
- Aggregate per person per month for the Art. 154(3)(c) notice.
- Framing the terms as a service or licence agreement supports both the 154(1)(d) classification and the economic-activity deduction (no Art. 97(1)(b)–(c) profit tax).
- Pay only after KYC. Without a personal number, STIFF cannot file the per-person report.
- For non-residents, collect the foreign tax ID, country of residence and where the task was performed, to support the Art. 104 source analysis.

### Gaps
- No source was found on whether Georgian civil law, or the Revenue Service, accepts electronic clickwrap for service agreements with individuals for tax-documentation purposes. This is common practice but unverified.
- Accounting-law (financial reporting) record-retention periods were not researched.

## 4. Personal data: the Law of Georgia on Personal Data Protection (No. 3144, 2023)

### Takeaway
The new PDP Law (adopted 14 June 2023, mostly in force from 1 March 2024) closely tracks the GDPR. Its main points:

- Ten lawful bases.
- Consent at 16; parental consent below 16, in writing for special-category data.
- Biometric data is special category.
- A 72-hour breach notice to the Service.
- Mandatory DPO for large-scale processing or systematic behaviour monitoring.
- DPIA for high-risk processing.
- Cross-border transfers allowed to "adequate" jurisdictions or on listed grounds. Contract-based transfers need a Service permit.

Fines are modest, in the low thousands of GEL per violation.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption and entry into force**
- Law No. 3144-XIმს-Xმპ, adopted 14/06/2023, published 03/07/2023. — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Main provisions in force 1 March 2024 (Art. 90(2)). Arts. 31 (DPIA), 33 (DPO), 80 and 82 (the related fines) in force 1 June 2024 (Art. 90(3)). Art. 6(1)(r)–(s) in force 1 January 2025 (Art. 90(4)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Lawful bases and special categories**
- Ten lawful grounds (Art. 5), including consent, contract, legal obligation and legitimate interests. The controller must justify the basis it relies on (Art. 5(2)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Special categories include biometric and genetic data used for unique identification (Art. 3(b)). They need safeguards plus a ground such as explicit consent (Art. 6(1)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Biometric processing only for listed purposes that cannot be achieved by other means (Art. 9(1)). Before processing, the controller must define in writing the purpose, storage period and protection mechanisms (Art. 9(2)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Minors**
- From age 16 the minor's own consent suffices; below 16, a parent or legal representative must consent (Art. 7). Special-category data of minors requires written parental consent (Art. 7(3)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307); consistent with [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=collection-and-processing&c=GE)

**Monitoring and marketing**
- Video monitoring must be adequate and proportionate (Art. 10(1)). Warning signs are required (Art. 10(8)–(9)). Audio monitoring needs a ground plus prior warning (Art. 11). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Direct marketing needs consent; data beyond name, address, phone and email needs written consent. Withdrawal must be honoured within 7 working days, and consent records kept for the marketing period plus 1 year (Art. 12). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Data subject rights**
- Information and access: 10 working days, extendable by 10 (Arts. 13–14).
- Rectification and erasure: 10 working days (Arts. 15–16).
- Blocking: decision within 3 working days (Art. 17).
- Portability (Art. 18); rights on automated decisions (Art. 19).
- Withdrawal of consent: processing stops within 10 working days (Art. 20).
- Complaints to the Service or a court (Art. 22).
- — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Records, DPIA, DPO, breaches**
- Records of processing (Art. 28). No general obligation to notify the Service of each processing activity was found in the text. — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- DPIA where processing poses high risk (Art. 31(1)). Mandatory for (Art. 31(2)): fully automated decisions with significant effects; large-scale special-category processing; systematic large-scale monitoring in public places. A "significant number" of data subjects means at least 3% of Georgia's population (Art. 31(7)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- A DPO is mandatory for public bodies, insurers, banks, microfinance organisations, credit bureaus, telecoms operators, airlines, airports and medical institutions. It is also mandatory for controllers processing large volumes of data or systematically monitoring behaviour (Art. 33). The appointment must be reported to the Service within 10 working days (Art. 33(8)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Breach: register the incident and notify the Service within **72 hours** (Art. 29(1)). Processors notify controllers immediately (Art. 29(2)). Data subjects must be notified where significant damage is highly probable, without unreasonable delay (Art. 30(1)). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307); consistent with [REVERA](https://georgia.revera.legal/en/info-centr/news-and-analytical-materials/382-soblyudenie-zakonodatelstva-o-zashhite-personalnyx-dannyx-v-gruzii-/)

**Processors and transfers**
- Processors need a written agreement covering purposes, categories, duration, instructions, confidentiality, security, and deletion or return of data (Art. 36). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- Cross-border transfers are allowed to adequate jurisdictions (Art. 37(1)). Otherwise they need a listed ground: an international treaty, a contract, informed written consent, vital interests, or public interest (Art. 37(2)). **Contract-based transfers require a Service permit** (Art. 37(3)). The Service maintains the adequacy list and reviews it at least every 3 years (Art. 38). — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Fines (GEL)**
- Each pair is base / aggravated, first for small entities (turnover up to GEL 500,000), then for larger ones:
  - unlawful processing (Art. 67): 1,000/2,000, or 2,000/4,000;
  - special-category data without grounds (Art. 68): 2,000/3,000, or 3,000/5,000;
  - no breach notice to the Service (Art. 78): 2,000/3,000, or 3,000/5,000;
  - no notice to data subjects (Art. 79): 3,000/5,000, or 5,000/10,000;
  - unlawful transfer (Art. 85): 2,000/4,000, or 4,000/6,000.
- No DPO (Art. 82): a warning first, then 3,000 if not fixed within 1 year.
- Aggravating factors include minors' data and large volumes (Art. 62).
- Fines for multiple offences in one proceeding are capped at 10,000 for small entities and 20,000 for larger ones (Art. 64).
- — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)
- An Art. 83 fine exists for failure to appoint a "special representative" (warning or 3,000; 5,000 if repeated). This suggests a representative obligation for certain controllers, probably foreign ones. — [PDP Law, matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307)

**Other**
- Law on Advertising, Art. 4(9): "Usage of an image or the name of a natural person in an advertisement without his/her consent shall be prohibited." — [Law on Advertising (English, matsne text)](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- GDPR applies to a non-EU controller processing personal data of data subjects in the EU where processing relates to offering goods or services to them, or to monitoring their behaviour in the EU (GDPR Art. 3(2)). — [Regulation (EU) 2016/679, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (primary text; not re-fetched this session)

### Inferences
- **Lawful bases:**
  - contract (Art. 5) for account data, task submissions and payouts;
  - legal obligation for tax KYC: personal number, name and address under Tax Code Art. 154(3)(c);
  - consent for marketing reuse of player videos and for direct marketing (Art. 12).
- **ID document photos** are not special category by themselves. A **selfie or face-match liveness check is biometric**, so it triggers Art. 6 and Art. 9. Prefer bank-account-name matching or a manual ID review over automated face matching. If biometrics are used, document the purpose, retention and protections in writing beforehand (Art. 9(2)) and run a DPIA.
- **Minors:** paying minors creates tax and civil-capacity problems as well as data issues: parental consent below 16, and written parental consent for any biometric data. The simplest compliant design is 18+ only for paid tasks. Under-16 participation needs verifiable parental consent.
- **DPO and DPIA:** a game that systematically tracks player behaviour (scores, anti-cheat, review queue) at scale arguably falls under the Art. 33 "systematic monitoring of behaviour" trigger. Appointing a DPO (internal or outsourced) and notifying the Service within 10 working days is the low-risk choice. The DPIA trigger "at least 3% of Georgia's population" (about 110,000 people, using a population of about 3.7 million) is unlikely to be met early on. The general high-risk DPIA under Art. 31(1) may still apply because of ID documents, bank data and possible biometrics.
- **Third parties in videos:** players filming "dares" in public will capture bystanders. The PDP Law's video-monitoring article targets monitoring systems, not user-generated content. Still, publishing identifiable bystanders in STIFF advertising conflicts with Advertising Law Art. 4(9) (consent for use of a person's image). Require blurring, or bystander releases for any reuse in marketing.
- **Hosting outside Georgia** (Supabase, Fly.io, Cloudflare):
  - Transfers are lawful if the destination is on the Service's adequacy list. Whether the US or EU are on it, and which processor regions are used, needs checking.
  - Otherwise the realistic grounds are a Service-permitted contract (Art. 37(3)), or informed written consent from players about the risks (Art. 37(2)).
  - A processor agreement under Art. 36 is required either way.
- **No registration:** no general controller-registration or notification duty was found in the 2023 text (the old 2011 law had a filing-system catalogue). The duties found are DPO notification and incident notification.
- **GDPR:** if STIFF targets EU residents (EU-language UI, EUR payouts, marketing aimed at the EU), Art. 3(2) applies. That would add an EU representative (GDPR Art. 27), EU-standard transfer rules and much larger fines. Geo-restricting paid tasks to Georgia avoids most of this.

### Gaps
- Amendments in the consolidated versions dated 29/05/2024 to 10/06/2026 were not read. One search result mentions "Law of Georgia No 1054 of 12 November 2025", which may amend this law, but this was not verified.
- The territorial-scope article (Art. 3) and the "special representative" obligation were not read in full.
- The Service's adequacy list and its normative acts on incident criteria, DPIA criteria and DPO exemption categories (required by Art. 88(4)) were not reviewed.
- personaldata.ge and pdps.ge could not be reached (connection refused).

## 5. Advertising law: sponsored content and promoting dares

### Takeaway
The Law of Georgia on Advertising (1998, last amended 25/06/2026) requires advertising to be recognisable as such without special knowledge. It bans advertising that urges people to commit dangerous actions harmful to health or safety, and requires consent to use a person's image. It also protects minors from depictions in dangerous places and circumstances. These provisions apply directly to paid player videos that STIFF reposts, and to dare-style task design. No influencer-specific statute was found.

### Cited Findings
- Advertising is defined as information "disseminated by any means and form that is intended for an unlimited group of persons" that promotes goods, persons or ideas (Art. 3(1)). — [Law on Advertising (English)](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Art. 4(1): "Upon advertising, the information shall be immediately perceived, without special knowledge or technical means, as an advertisement." — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Art. 4(11): an advertisement "must not incite citizens to violence, aggression or chaos, and must not urge them to commit dangerous actions that may harm human health or threaten his/her safety." — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Art. 4(2): advertising in the official language (Georgian) throughout Georgia, with exceptions. Art. 4(9): no use of a person's image or name without consent. — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Art. 14 protects minors. It bars, among other things, using "text or audio and video information that displays minors in dangerous places and circumstances" (14(c)). — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Liability: the advertising customer is liable for content; producers and disseminators are liable for their parts (Art. 20). Injured persons may sue for damages and public refutation (Art. 21(3)). The Communications Commission (ComCom) enforces only broadcast advertising (Art. 21(2^1)). — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)
- Latest amendment: Law No. 1826, 25/06/2026, published 03/07/2026. Its content was not read. — [matsne, Law on Advertising](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31840)
- The Tax Code refers to "sponsorship by the Law of Georgia on Advertising" (Art. 8 definitions). Sponsorship is regulated in Art. 13 of the Advertising Law. — [Tax Code](https://alservice.ge/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tax-code-2023.pdf); [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)

### Inferences
- Player videos that STIFF pays for and publishes, or that players post with STIFF branding, are likely "advertising" under Art. 3(1). They should carry a clear marker such as "#რეკლამა / #ad / paid by STIFF" to satisfy Art. 4(1).
- **Dares:** STIFF is the "advertising customer" setting the content, so under Art. 20(1) it bears content liability. Tasks that encourage risky stunts, trespass, traffic danger or harassment of strangers risk breaching Art. 4(11). They may also create civil and criminal exposure beyond advertising law. A written task-safety policy and moderation reject rules are the mitigation.
- No GEL fine amounts for non-broadcast advertising violations were found in the Advertising Law itself. Liability is "under the legislation of Georgia", meaning the Administrative Offences Code and civil claims.

## 6. Company setup: a separate LLC, Virtual Zone / International Company status, and gambling-law scope

### Takeaway
International Company and Virtual Zone status are designed for IT and maritime service exporters selling to foreign clients. They do not fit a consumer game run for Georgian players and funded by STIFF's own marketing budget. A separate LLC for the game is a legitimate liability ring-fence, but it adds its own withholding agent and reporting. A skill or task game with no chance element appears to fall outside the lottery and gambling permit regime. Introducing random draws would change that.

### Cited Findings
- **Virtual Zone status:** 0% profit tax and 0% VAT on revenue from non-Georgian entities, with 5% on distributed dividends. It requires a Georgian company providing software-related services that invoices foreign clients. — [IBCCS](https://ibccs.tax/blog/virtual-zone-vs-international-company-status-georgia/); [Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-s-virtual-zone-status-for-it-companies-the-complete-2025-guide-to-tax-benefits) (secondary consultancy sources)
- **International Company status:** 5% profit tax on the Estonian-model object. Eligibility is stricter: about two years of experience in qualifying IT or maritime services, plus staff and office substance. Sources disagree on the personal income tax and dividend details. — [Legalese](https://legalese.ge/services/international-company-status-in-georgia/); [IBCCS](https://ibccs.tax/blog/virtual-zone-vs-international-company-status-georgia/) (secondary)
- **Lotteries law (Law of Georgia on Organising Lotteries, Games of Chance and Other Prize Games), read in its 2016–2017 consolidated text:**
  - A "promotional game" is one where the operator draws prize money under predetermined published terms and "the selection of a winning ticket shall be determined by a chance event" (Art. 3(n.d)).
  - Tickets must be free, and no payment for participation is allowed (Art. 3(n.d)).
  - A permit is required (Arts. 5(1), 11(1)(a)), is issued by the Revenue Service (Art. 7(1)) and lasts one year (Art. 11(3)).
  - Art. 4: games "which are not determined by a chance event, may not be construed as lotteries, games of chance or other prize games". These include games for demonstrating "special knowledge, intelligence, prowess".
  - — [matsne, Lotteries law](https://www.matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/30988)
- Law on Advertising Art. 4(6): advertising an activity that requires a permit not yet obtained is inadmissible. — [Law on Advertising](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Georgia/Georgia-Law-on-Advertising.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Virtual Zone / IC status: not relevant.** The game's "income" is a marketing cost for STIFF, and its participants are domestic individuals. Neither regime exempts STIFF's withholding on payouts to Georgian individuals. This confirms the expectation, though it rests on secondary sources.
- **Separate LLC:**
  - Advantages: ring-fences tort and claims risk from dares, data breaches and player disputes, away from the shop and its customer data. Also separates the PDP controller role and gives cleaner books for the payout ledger.
  - Costs: separate monthly withholding filings; intra-group funding (STIFF paying the game LLC for marketing services is a taxable service with VAT if registered); transfer-pricing-style scrutiny of the related-party fee; and a separate DPO, processor agreements and records.
  - Under the Estonian model, funding the game LLC through a capital contribution rather than a service fee avoids VAT, but shifts expenses into a company with no revenue. Inside that company the payouts are still economic-activity expenses, if they are documented as services.
  - Recommendation: get advice from a Georgian accountant or lawyer before restructuring.
- **Gambling or permit risk:** keep tasks skill or effort-based with deterministic rewards, and charge no entry fee. Any random element (mystery boxes, prize draws, random coin multipliers) risks the "promotional game" or "game of chance" characterisation, which brings a Revenue Service permit and, for gambling, far heavier regulation. Under Art. 154(1)(e) the PIT rate on promotional-game winnings would still be 20%.

### Gaps
- The current (2026) consolidated text of the Lotteries law was not read. Article numbers and the permit-fee rules may have changed since 2017.
- No official Revenue Service or Ministry of Finance source confirming 2026 International Company or Virtual Zone eligibility was found. All sources are consultancies.
- Liability ring-fencing under the Georgian Law on Entrepreneurs (veil-piercing standards) was not researched.
