# Georgian licensing, payments and AML regime for a real-money task-reward game (STIFF LLC)

Scope note: research run 2026-11-20. Primary texts were read on matsne.gov.ge where the page loaded. Matsne often served an older consolidated version or was truncated. Each point says which version was read. Nothing here is legal advice. Every "Interpretation" item needs confirmation by a Georgian lawyer.

## 1. Gambling and prize games: is the game a game of chance, prize game, lottery or promotional draw?

### Takeaway
Read as written, the current game is **none of the regulated categories**. Rewards are fixed, set by the operator and earned by doing a task. No winner is drawn by chance, and entry is free. The law's own carve-out (Art. 4) excludes games decided by skill, knowledge or dexterity "with no element of chance". Three later features could pull the game into the permit regime: randomly drawn rewards, sponsor or finale prizes allocated by draw, and paid coins that buy a chance to win money. A free brand raffle is a "promotional draw" that needs a Revenue Service permit (reported fee GEL 15,000, valid 1 year). A draw with paid entry cannot be a promotional draw at all.

### Cited Findings
- The law is the Law of Georgia on Organising Lotteries, Games of Chance and Other Prize Games, No. 1180, adopted 25 March 2005, published 19 April 2005. Matsne shows consolidated versions up to 28/10/2026. — [Matsne, doc 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 3(ა), gambling game (აზარტული თამაშობა):** the outcome depends wholly or partly on chance and cash winnings are possible. Examples are cards, dice (not backgammon), roulette and slot machines. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 3(თ), lottery:** a voluntary group or mass game in which the organiser publicly draws a prize fund under published rules. Winning is by chance and cannot be arranged. Subtypes are ticket draws and instant lotteries. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 3(ო), prize game (მომგებიანი თამაშობა):** lotto, bingo and totalizator (bets on the outcome of an event). — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 3(ო.დ), promotional draw (წამახალისებელი გათამაშება):**
  - The prize fund is drawn publicly under published rules, and winning is by chance.
  - Tickets are issued **free**, and charging any fee for participation is **not permitted**.
  - The permit goes to the producer of a specific product or service, or its representative, unless the Government decides otherwise. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 4:** games whose outcome depends on skill, knowledge or dexterity, with no element of chance, are not lotteries or gambling games. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 5:**
  - 5(1): every gambling or prize game, every online ("systemic-electronic") organisation of one, and every supply of one needs a permit under the Law on Licences and Permits.
  - 5(2): only games permitted by this law may be held.
  - 5(2¹): promotional draws tied to tobacco are banned.
  - 5(3): the Government may assign promotional draws to the Revenue Service. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 11:**
  - 11(1): the permit types include promotional draws.
  - 11(2): permits go only to businesses registered in Georgia.
  - 11(3): lotteries and promotional draws may run electronically only on this law's conditions. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Promotional draw mechanics:**
  - Art. 9(3): no statutory cap on the prize fund.
  - Art. 12(1)(ბ.ზ): the rules must state the prize fund, the draw date and address, and the prize list.
  - Art. 13(3)-(5): rules cannot change during a stage, and each new stage needs prior approval.
  - Art. 16: the rules must be published, a draw committee formed, and results reported within 2 days.
  - Art. 18(ბ): the permit lasts 1 year. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Age, Art. 32(1):** Georgian citizens under 25 and foreigners or stateless persons under 18 may not take part in gambling, including online. **Promotional draws are excluded** from this bar. Art. 32(2): lottery tickets may not be sold to, and prizes may not be paid to, persons under 18. — [Matsne 30988, offset 100000](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Art. 32(1¹)** applies the self-exclusion ("dependent persons") list and the prohibited-persons list, again excluding promotional draws. **Art. 35** sets location bans, also excluding promotional draws. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Sanctions:**
  - Art. 37²(2)-(3): an unauthorised lottery is fined GEL 50,000, and GEL 150,000 for each repeat. The Revenue Service imposes the fine (Art. 37³).
  - Art. 37¹: permit-condition fines, for example GEL 20,000 for a casino.
  - Art. 29(1)(b): winnings must be reported to the FMS. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **The 2021 amendments (Law No. 1185 of 22 Dec 2021):** in force from 1 Feb and 1 Mar 2022. They raised the citizens' age to 25, barred civil servants, socially vulnerable persons and self-excluded persons, and banned gambling advertising. — [identomat compliance guide](https://www.identomat.com/compliance-guide/guide/igaming-gambling-compliance-in-georgia); [Conse](https://conse.ge/en/publications/8/satamasho-biznesis-shesakheb-akhali-regulaciebi-dzalashi-shedis)
- **Advertising ban (from 1 March 2022):**
  - It covers ads for games of chance, betting, lotto, bingo and their organisers, in all forms including electronic networks.
  - Broadcasting may not promote games of chance or prize games, **except promotional games**.
  - Fines are GEL 10,000, then GEL 20,000 for a repeat, and triple if not corrected within a week.
  - The organiser fine for admitting an underage player is GEL 10,000, then GEL 20,000.
  - Promotional games cannot be run by an organiser that holds a permit for games of chance, betting, lotto or bingo, "except for cases set by the legislation". — [Justice League](https://www.justiceleague.ge/blog/gambling-industry)
- **Fees reported by secondary sources:**
  - Promotional prize draw permit: Revenue Service, 1 year, GEL 15,000.
  - Lotto and bingo permits: 5 years, GEL 15,000 per year. — [GC Authority](https://gcauthority.com/markets/georgia/)
- **Fees reported by Andersen in Georgia (Legal 500, 2026):**
  - Online casino: GEL 5,000,000 a year.
  - Online slot salon: GEL 1,000,000.
  - Online sportsbook: GEL 100,000 on top of a land-based sportsbook permit, to which it must be tied.
  - International systemic-electronic permit (2026 amendments): GEL 100,000 more.
  - Review takes up to 20 working days.
  - Crypto is not allowed. — [Legal 500 / Andersen](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/georgia-gambling-law/)
- **Hybrid products:** Andersen says unapproved "skill-chance hybrid products" are prohibited. — [Legal 500 / Andersen](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/georgia-gambling-law/)

### Inferences
- **Core design, outside the law (Interpretation, high confidence, lawyer to confirm).** A fixed GEL amount for an approved task involves no chance draw, no prize fund and no stake. That is work or a performance reward, not a game whose outcome turns on chance. The Art. 4 exclusion fits it well.
- **Risk point: discretionary review.** Human or AI review is a judgment on performance, not chance. Keep the approval criteria published and objective so that review cannot be called arbitrary or chance-like.
- **Watcher voting.** Small fixed rewards for voting fall outside the law. If voters are paid only when they match the majority, that is closer to a prediction or bet on an outcome, which resembles a totalizator under Art. 3(ო.გ). Avoid pari-mutuel or pooled payouts. Pay a fixed amount per vote, or pay for accuracy against the final ground-truth verdict, with no stake.
- **Paid coins.** If coins buy only cosmetics or features, and never raise the chance of a cash prize, the law likely stays out of it. If paid coins buy entries into a draw, or loot-box-style random cash or prizes, that is stake + chance + prize, which is gambling or a lottery. A promotional-draw permit is then impossible (Art. 3(ო.დ) bans entry fees), and the full gambling regime applies: Georgian citizens 25+, the advertising ban, and permit fees in the hundreds of thousands to millions of GEL.
- **Paid entry to tasks.** A paid entry fee with a skill-based fixed reward is a contest-for-pay. It is not expressly covered, but it is high-risk under Andersen's warning on hybrids. Keep the game free.
- **Season finale or sponsor prizes allocated by draw.** These need a promotional-draw permit:
  - Entry must be free.
  - The organiser should be STIFF as the producer of its own product, or a sponsor as producer.
  - The rules must be approved.
  - A draw committee and reporting are required.
  - The reported fee is GEL 15,000 a year.

  Prizes awarded purely on leaderboard performance (skill) arguably need no permit. A tie-break by lot reintroduces chance.
- Under-18 players can take part in a promotional draw (it is excluded from Art. 32(1)). However, Art. 32(2) bars paying lottery prizes to under-18s. Whether that applies to promotional draws was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- The full English consolidated text (Oct 2026) of Arts. 3, 4, 32 and 35 was not read verbatim. Article numbering came from a machine translation of the Georgian page, and Art. 4's exact wording should be checked.
- Promotional-draw fee: the Law on Licence and Permit Fees (No. 2937/2003) was not read. The GEL 15,000 figure rests only on a secondary site.
- The advertising-ban article number was not found in the text read.
- No Revenue Service guidance or ruling on skill-based or task-reward apps was found.
- No firm brief from BLC, Dentons, BGI, Nodia Urumashvili, MKD or Kavkasia on promotional games was found in this run.

## 2. Payment services and e-money: does holding balances make STIFF a PSP or e-money issuer?

### Takeaway
Under the Law on Payment Systems and Payment Services, only an NBG-registered PSP, a bank, a microfinance organisation or an exempt entity may provide payment services (Art. 15). Issuing e-money is a payment service (Art. 13(1)(f)). Settled law and interpretation separate on one question: whether STIFF's balance is "e-money". It probably is not if the balance is STIFF's own debt for rewards earned, never funded by users, and redeemable only by STIFF paying out. The risk rises sharply once users load money (bought coins) or can transfer balances to each other. The cleanest model routes payouts through a licensed PSP or bank partner, either paying out at once or holding funds in the partner's safeguarded account.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 2:**
  - PSP: a legal person providing payment services to users.
  - E-money provider: a PSP that issues e-money.
  - E-money: "the nominal value of funds received by an electronic money provider from users", stored electronically and accepted as a means of payment by others. — [Matsne 1673253, version 22/12/2021](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **Art. 13(1):** the payment services include issuing e-money and executing e-money transactions (13(1)(f)), plus money remittance and the issuing and acquiring of payment instruments. — [Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **Art. 13(2) exclusions:** direct cash payments; payments through a commercial agent; technical or IT service providers that never hold the funds; instruments usable only on the issuer's premises; digital purchases delivered through the same device; intra-group payments; cheques. The version read had no general "limited network/loyalty" exclusion. — [Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **Art. 15:** payment services may be provided only by an NBG-registered PSP, a commercial bank, a microfinance organisation or an Art. 14(1) person. Significant PSPs must meet NBG minimum capital. — [Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **Art. 18:**
  - User funds go in a nominee account separate from own funds (18(2)).
  - No interest on e-money (18(8)).
  - E-money is the provider's obligation to the user (18(10)).
  - Legal persons may take part in e-money schemes only as payees (18(11)). — [Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **Art. 17(1):** "significant PSP" status depends on NBG-set limits on e-money issued or turnover. **Art. 43(7):** records are kept at least 15 years. — [Matsne 1673253](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1673253)
- **NBG orders:** registration rules are in Order N77/04 (May 2023). Capital rules are in Order N79/04, in force 1 Jan 2024, with minimum supervisory capital of **GEL 50,000 to 350,000 depending on the scope of services**. — [BGI Legal brief, May 2023](https://bgi.ge/files/BGI%20Law%20Brief%20_%20May%202023.pdf) (as summarised in search; the PDF was not opened)
- **Conflicting secondary figures:**
  - A flat GEL 250,000 minimum. — [licentium](https://www.licentium.io/hub/georgia)
  - About GEL 1,000,000 for an e-money institution. — [legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/banking-and-finance-law-en/e-money-regulation-en)
  - Treat all three as unverified against the order text.

### Inferences
- **Current model: reward ledger, STIFF pays out from its own bank account (Interpretation, medium confidence).**
  - Users fund nothing, so no "funds received from users" exist and the balance is arguably not e-money under Art. 2.
  - Paying a user their own earned reward is a payment for STIFF's own account, not a service to third parties.
  - Two risks: the balance becomes transferable between users, or is accepted by third parties. Either would meet "accepted as a means of payment by others".
  - Keep the balance non-transferable, redeemable only by withdrawal to the user's own card, and described in the ToS as STIFF's accrued payable.
- **Paid coins (Interpretation).** Coins bought with money and spendable only on STIFF's own features are close to the "usable only on the issuer's premises" exclusion in Art. 13(2). The risk appears if paid coins can be converted to withdrawable GEL, which makes them e-money-like stored value funded by users. Never allow bought coins to be cashed out. Keep two separate currencies: earned GEL and bought coins.
- **Loyalty-points model.** Converting tasks into points redeemable only for STIFF products (clothing) removes payment-law risk. The trade-off is that it gives up cash rewards.
- **Immediate payout (no stored balance).** Each approved task triggers a card payout through the acquirer or bank's push-to-card API. This minimises stored-value questions. Very small amounts make fees per transaction costly, so batching at a threshold is common, and batching re-creates a short-held balance. Either way the money still moves as STIFF's own payout, which is the stronger position.
- **Licensed partner holding funds.** Use a Georgian bank or registered PSP (wallet, payout or marketplace product) to hold user balances in a safeguarded or nominee account. STIFF then only instructs payouts, which shifts the Art. 15 and Art. 18 duties to the partner. This is the recommended structure if balances become large or paid features arrive.
- **Own PSP registration** would need NBG registration under N77/04, GEL 50k to 350k capital under N79/04 (if accurate), safeguarding, AML obliged-entity status and 15-year records. That is disproportionate for a fashion brand.

### Gaps
- The current (2026) consolidated payment law was not read; matsne served the 2021 version. Later versions to 15/10/2026 exist and may have added EU PSD2-style limited-network or loyalty exclusions.
- NBG Orders N77/04 and N79/04 were not read. No source gave registration timelines.
- No NBG guidance on reward or cash-back balances was found.

## 3. AML/CFT: is STIFF an obliged entity?

### Takeaway
The 2019 AML Law (No. 5226-Iს, consolidated with amendments to 01/04/2026) lists the organisers of lotteries, gambling and other prize games, and PSPs, as obliged entities. A fashion brand running a skill-based reward game with no permit is **probably not** an obliged entity. That changes if STIFF obtains a promotional-draw or gambling permit, or registers as a PSP. Even outside the list, Georgian banks and PSP partners will apply their own KYC to STIFF's payouts. The reported gambling CDD trigger is GEL 5,000 per transaction.

### Cited Findings
- **The law:** Law of Georgia on Facilitating the Prevention of Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism, No. 5226-Iს, 30/10/2019. The latest amendment listed is 01/04/2026 (1460-Vმს-XIმპ). — [Matsne 4690334](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4690334)
- **May 2023 amendments:** obliged entities include "(i) organisers of lotteries, gambling or other commercial games". — [MONEYVAL 2024 follow-up report](https://rm.coe.int/moneyval-2024-22-ge-5thround-3rdenhfur/1680b2c262)
- **CDD trigger:** gambling, lottery and quiz organisers perform CDD when accepting funds or paying winnings above GEL 5,000 (or the foreign-currency equivalent), and on establishing a business relationship. — [identomat](https://www.identomat.com/compliance-guide/guide/igaming-gambling-compliance-in-georgia); [georgiawealth.info](https://georgiawealth.info/compliance-in-georgia/)
- **Threshold reporting:** reportedly, by FMS head order in force October 2020, receipts and payouts above GEL 50,000 are reported within 5 working days (single secondary source). An older figure of GEL 30,000 appears in FMS reports. — [identomat](https://www.identomat.com/compliance-guide/guide/igaming-gambling-compliance-in-georgia); [FMS Annual Report 2019](https://fms.gov.ge/Uploads/files/ENG_Annual_Report_2019_20.05.20.pdf)
- **Gambling-law reporting:** Art. 29(1)(b) of the gambling law requires winnings to be reported to the FMS. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)
- **Duties of obliged gambling operators:** risk-based CDD, enhanced due diligence and PEP checks, sanctions screening, suspicious-transaction reports to the FMS, an AML officer, internal policies and independent audit. — [Legal 500 / Andersen](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/georgia-gambling-law/)
- **Supervisors:** the NBG supervises its obliged entities (banks, PSPs). Sources disagree on gambling: the Ministry of Finance or the Revenue Service. — [NBG AML supervision](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/aml-cft-supervisi-1); [Andersen AML for gambling](https://ge.andersen.com/aml-georgia-gambling/)

### Inferences
- **No permit, no PSP status:** STIFF is likely outside Art. 3 (Interpretation). Even so, the following are recommended as good practice and because the bank partner will require them: a voluntary KYC tier, a name match between the card holder and the account holder, velocity limits and fraud monitoring (multi-accounting is the real risk).
- **With a promotional-draw permit:** STIFF may arguably become an "organiser of other prize games" and so an obliged entity. Confirm with counsel and the FMS.
- Identity verification before a first withdrawal also supports age checks, the tax position on payments to individuals (not researched here) and anti-fraud.

### Gaps
- The text of Art. 3 and the CDD articles of the current AML law was not read (the matsne page served metadata only, and the FMS PDF failed TLS).
- The GEL 3,000 or 15,000 occasional-transaction thresholds for other entity types were not verified.
- The current FMS threshold-reporting order was not read.

## 4. Minors (16-17)

### Takeaway
In Georgia majority is at 18 (Civil Code Art. 12(2)), and 7-18 year olds have limited capacity (Art. 14(1)). A minor's transaction generally needs a legal representative's consent unless it only brings a benefit (Arts. 15, 63). Receiving a reward is arguably a pure benefit, but accepting ToS (data processing, release of liability, filming dares) is not, so **parental consent is needed for 16-17s**. A safer design makes the cash version 18+, or requires verified parental consent and the payout to a parent's card.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 12(2):** majority at 18. **Art. 12(3):** marriage before 18 gives capacity. — [Civil Code, Matsne 31702](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- **Art. 14(1):** ages 7 to 18 have limited capacity. — [Matsne 31702](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- **Art. 15:** a declaration of intent needs the representative's consent "except when the person with limited legal capacity acquires a benefit from the transaction". — [Matsne 31702](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- **Art. 63(1):** a bilateral contract without consent depends on later approval, except where it is a benefit. **Art. 64(1):** the other party may repudiate before approval. **Art. 66:** a unilateral transaction without consent is void. — [Matsne 31702](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- **Art. 65:**
  - 65(1): a contract is valid if the minor performs it with means given for their free disposal.
  - 65(2)-(3): a minor of 16 authorised to run an enterprise has full capacity for routine matters in it. That needs representative consent plus approval from the guardianship authority. — [Matsne 31702](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702)
- **Gambling law Art. 32(2):** a ban on paying lottery prizes to under-18s. — [Matsne 30988](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/30988)

### Inferences
- The fixed-reward game is not gambling, so the 25+ and 18+ gambling age bars do not apply as such. Any promotional or season draw, however, faces the Art. 32(2) question for under-18s.
- Dares filmed by minors raise child-safety, data and image-rights concerns, and harm liability falls more heavily on the operator.
- Card issuance is set by the bank. Under-18 cards generally depend on the bank's own product rules (unverified).
- **Recommendation (Interpretation):** run the money version 18+ with ID-verified KYC at withdrawal. If 16-17 is kept: verified parental consent, a parent's card for payouts, a restricted task pool (no physical-risk dares), and stricter content moderation.

### Gaps
- The Code on the Rights of the Child (2019) was not read in this run, so its articles on child protection, economic exploitation and digital safety are not cited.
- The Labour Code rules on employing 16-17 year olds were not read. This is relevant if task rewards are seen as remuneration for work.
- The Communications Commission (ComCom) rules on minors' protection were not read. They mainly bind broadcasters and authorised services, so their applicability to an app is uncertain.
- The Personal Data Protection Law (2023) on children's consent was not researched.

## 6. Liability for harm during dares

### Takeaway
Not verified in primary sources in this run. This is flagged as a high-priority gap: an operator that sets and pays for dares is exposed to civil liability, and possibly to criminal provisions on inciting dangerous acts. A lawyer must review the task pool.

### Cited Findings
- None verified in this run.

### Inferences
- **Civil risk (Interpretation):** under general tort principles in the Civil Code (articles not read), an operator that designs and rewards a task that foreseeably causes injury or property damage may be liable. ToS waivers are unlikely to protect against harm to minors or third parties.
- **Criminal risk (Interpretation):** dares involving public-order offences, trespass, filming third parties without consent, or self-harm could expose STIFF under incitement, complicity or minors' endangerment provisions of the Criminal Code (not read).
- **Mitigations:**
  - A curated, pre-approved task pool with no physical-risk, public-disorder, third-party or alcohol and drug tasks.
  - Rejection plus reporting of unsafe hand-ins.
  - Consent of anyone filmed.
  - No escalating "harder pays more" dares that reward danger.

### Gaps
- The Civil Code tort articles (Art. 992 and following, numbering unverified) were not read.
- The Criminal Code provisions (incitement to suicide, endangering minors, hooliganism) were not read.
- No Georgian case law on challenge or dare apps was found.
